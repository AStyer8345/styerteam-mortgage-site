What Is a 1099-Only Mortgage in Texas?

A 1099-only mortgage is a Non-QM loan designed specifically for sole proprietors, 1099 independent contractors, tech consultants, and real estate agents. While conventional loans evaluate net profit after all business expenses on IRS Form 1040 Schedule C, 1099 programs start from your gross 1099 earnings.

Underwriters apply a flat 10% expense factor to your gross 1099 total (crediting 90% as qualifying income) and divide by 12 or 24 months. You provide your 1099 statements and proof that you are still working with the payor or in the same line of work. No tax returns, transcripts, or detailed bank audits are required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the questions self-employed borrowers ask most.

How does the 10% expense factor work on a 1099 loan?

Underwriters take your gross 1099 revenue and deduct 10% for estimated business expenses, crediting the remaining 90% as qualifying net income. For example, if you gross $150,000, your qualifying income is $135,000 ($11,250/month).

Do I need to submit tax returns or Schedule C?

No. On genuine 1099-only mortgage programs, tax returns, 1040s, and Schedule C are not requested or reviewed. Your qualifying income is established solely from your 1099 statements and proof of continuing business.

Can I qualify with only 1 year of 1099 history?

Yes! While many lenders prefer 2 years, we have wholesale programs that allow qualification based on a single year of 1099 earnings, provided you worked in the same line of work previously (e.g. transitioning from W-2 to 1099 in the same industry).

What credit score is required for a 1099 mortgage?

Minimum credit scores typically start at 620 to 660, with the best interest rates and highest loan-to-value (up to 90% LTV) available for credit scores of 720 and above.

Can 1099 loans be used for investment properties?

Yes. 1099-only mortgages can be used to purchase or refinance primary residences, second vacation homes, or residential 1–4 unit investment properties.
