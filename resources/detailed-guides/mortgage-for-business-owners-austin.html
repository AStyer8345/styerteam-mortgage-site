How should your mortgage fit the business?

A mortgage can affect more than a monthly payment. Decide how much liquidity to keep in the business, who owns and can access the funds, and whether an income change or transaction affects the timing. Those decisions should guide the financing review.

For the income calculation and documentation choices, start with self-employed mortgage qualification. Use this page for the related business-planning decisions.

Three planning questions to settle early.

How much cash should stay in the business?

Separate your down payment and reserves from payroll, taxes, operating expenses and upcoming business needs. A lender must also review whether business funds are accessible and whether their withdrawal affects operations.

How do ownership and compensation fit together?

Map each entity, your ownership share, wages, distributions and any business debts. Do not change compensation or tax reporting solely for a mortgage before reviewing the effects with your CPA and loan officer.

What will change before closing?

A business sale, new entity, expansion or property purchase can change income and available funds. Bring the dates and expected changes into the discussion before relying on today’s account balances.

Business revenue and mortgage qualifying income are different measures. If the tax-return calculation does not support the goal, compare an eligible deposit-based or P&L path rather than assuming the file is finished.

The easiest-sounding document list is not automatically the best financing choice. Before choosing a program, put the alternatives next to each other:

  2. How much cash will closing and reserves require?Consider the business’s operating needs as well as personal liquidity. Money in a business account is not automatically available for a home purchase.
  3. What are the total costs and ongoing obligations?Compare written quotes using the same property, loan amount, term and date. Include points, credits, mortgage insurance where applicable and any relevant prepayment terms.
  4. Can the file meet your timing?Identify what is already documented, what needs explanation and which property or business questions remain unresolved.

FAQ — Mortgage for Business Owners in Austin

Can a self-employed business owner in Austin get a mortgage without tax returns?

Yes. Bank statement, 1099, P&L and asset-depletion programs qualify you without tax-return income. Bank statement loans use 12 or 24 months of deposits with a 50% default expense factor; 1099 loans use gross 1099 income minus 10%.

Why do tax returns make me look poorer than I am as a business owner?

Taxable income and mortgage qualifying income can differ. A conventional review considers permitted adjustments, such as eligible add-backs, along with income history and business performance. An alternative method may use eligible deposits, a P&L, 1099 earnings, or assets. Compare the supported calculations before assuming the tax return rules out a suitable loan.

What is the 2026 conforming loan limit in Travis County?

The 2026 baseline conforming loan limit for a one-unit property in Travis County is $832,750, up $26,250 from 2025. No Texas county qualifies as high-cost in 2026, so the same one-unit limit applies statewide. A loan above the applicable conforming limit is a jumbo loan; whether it is a qualified mortgage or Non-QM is a separate classification. Source: FHFA 2026 Conforming Loan Limit Values.

What's the difference between bank statement, P&L, and 1099 loans?

Three ways to document the same business. Bank statement loans average 12 or 24 months of deposits minus an expense factor (50% by default). 1099 loans start from gross 1099 income with a flat 10% factor. P&L loans use a CPA-prepared profit-and-loss statement, with 700-plus credit and up to 80% loan-to-value.

How does asset depletion work for high-net-worth Austin borrowers?

Asset-depletion programs calculate qualifying income from eligible documented assets after required deductions. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac publish specific agency methods; non-QM asset eligibility, discounts, divisors, leverage, reserves, and documentation vary by investor and current program.

What is a DSCR loan and when does it beat a self-employed program?

A DSCR loan is generally a business-purpose investment-property mortgage that primarily evaluates qualifying rent relative to PITIA. Personal-income, credit, asset, reserve, guaranty, entity, rent, and property-document requirements vary by investor and program. Primary- and second-home borrowers need a different eligible loan type.

Can K-1 income from a partnership or S-corp qualify for a mortgage?

K-1 and entity income may qualify after a current analysis of ownership, distributions, business liquidity, income history, and required tax documents. The applicable agency or investor guide determines how income, losses, and eligible adjustments are treated.

What loan programs cover one-time-close custom construction in Texas?

A one-time-close structure combines construction and permanent financing at the initial closing. A two-time-close structure uses separate construction and permanent loans. Eligibility, loan size, builder, budget, payment, rate-lock, draw, completion, conversion, title, closing-cost, and legal requirements vary by lender, program, property, and transaction.

Will the rate be higher than a conventional loan?

Not necessarily. Pricing varies by date, program, credit, leverage, property, documentation, lock period, and other scenario details. Compare same-day written quotes or Loan Estimates using matching assumptions. A future refinance may be possible, but it is not guaranteed.

How fast does a self-employed mortgage typically close in Austin?

Once your documents are in, we review them and issue a pre-approval letter. P&L and jumbo files typically close in 25 to 35 days when documents, appraisal and title are clean.

Can my LLC or S-corp own the property?

For a rental, yes: DSCR loans allow LLC ownership. For a home you live in, title usually goes in your personal name or an eligible revocable trust. Ask your attorney and CPA about liability and tax effects before moving title.

How much down payment do business owners need?

From 10% on a primary residence with bank statement or 1099 programs on the strongest files, and 20% on P&L-only programs (80% maximum loan-to-value). Conventional loans follow their own published minimums when your tax returns support the loan.

Do I need to pay myself a bigger salary from my S-corp to qualify?

Start with a review of your existing compensation and business records before changing salary. Depending on the rules and ownership, a lender may consider wages and other eligible business income, with adjustments for access, distributions, business strength, and history. A higher paycheck alone does not establish more qualifying income. Coordinate any compensation change with your tax professional.

Can I use money from my business account for the down payment?

Usually, yes. Lenders accept business funds when you own the business and the withdrawal won’t hurt it, typically shown with a CPA letter or a review of business cash flow. Tell me early if the same business provides both your income and your down payment.

How far ahead of buying should I talk to a lender if I'm self-employed?

Start when the purchase becomes a realistic goal, especially before major business, compensation, or financing changes. An early review can identify records or history that still need to be established. If you are already looking at homes, share your timing now; the review will determine what can proceed and what needs more preparation.

What should I ask my CPA before I apply?

Ask which records explain current business earnings, whether planned entity or tax changes affect those records, and what preparation or verification the CPA can provide if requested. The mortgage professional should calculate qualifying income under the selected program; your CPA advises on taxes. With your permission, coordinating those questions can help avoid missing documentation.

I own several businesses. Which ones does the lender look at?

Provide a clear map of your entities, ownership, income, losses, and guarantees. The lender reviews businesses used for qualifying income and may need to consider other business losses or obligations even when their income is not being used. The program determines the treatment. Begin with a summary, then provide required returns and K-1s through secure collection.

My income dropped last year, or it's seasonal. Am I stuck?

A decline or seasonal pattern needs explanation, not an automatic conclusion. The lender will review current earnings, the reason for the change, and whether the income appears stable under the selected program. A longer average can help explain seasonality but does not cancel an ongoing decline, and a shorter statement period does not guarantee eligibility.

Do I have to give up my tax write-offs to buy a house?

Not necessarily. Begin with an accurate review of your existing returns and any permitted adjustments. Another documentation method may fit, but it still must support the loan under its own rules. Discuss deductions, amendments, and other tax decisions with your tax professional rather than changing them solely to pursue a mortgage calculation.
