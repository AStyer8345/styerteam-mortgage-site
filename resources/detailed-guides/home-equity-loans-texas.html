Texas Home Equity Loans: Keep Your Low Rate

A home equity loan (HELOAN) gives you a fixed-rate lump sum as a second mortgage, so your low first-mortgage rate stays in place. And you don’t need a W-2 to qualify: my programs accept bank statements, 1099s, a P&L or assets.

Home equity loans when your income doesn’t fit a W-2.

Most banks qualify a home equity loan the same way they qualify a first mortgage: tax returns and W-2s. If you’re self-employed, paid on 1099s or living on investments, that’s often where the answer becomes no. My home equity programs accept other ways to document income:

  • Bank statements: 12 or 24 months of deposits instead of tax returns, for business owners whose write-offs shrink their taxable income. How bank statement income works.
  • 1099 income: gross 1099 earnings for contractors and commission earners. 1099 qualification.
  • P&L: a CPA-prepared profit-and-loss statement. P&L qualification.
  • Assets: eligible savings and investments converted to qualifying income for retirees and high-net-worth borrowers. Asset-based qualification.

You keep your first mortgage and its rate. The new loan is the only thing priced at today’s rates. Tell me how you earn and I’ll match the right program.

Your first mortgage can stay in place.

If you have a mortgage rate you want to keep, a cash-out refinance is not the only option worth discussing. A second loan has its own balance, payment, and terms. It may help fund a planned home improvement or consolidate higher-interest debt without replacing the first loan.

The right choice depends on your goal, available equity, qualification, fees, and repayment plan. An equity loan adds a lien against your property. If you cannot make the payments, your home may be at risk.

Texas home equity loan questions

What is a home equity loan (HELOAN)?

A home equity loan is a second mortgage that pays you a lump sum at a fixed rate, with its own fixed monthly payment. Your first mortgage stays exactly as it is. It fits a planned expense like a renovation, paying off higher-rate debt or funding a purchase.

Should I use a home equity loan or a cash-out refinance?

If your first-mortgage rate is lower than today’s rates, a home equity loan usually wins: you only pay the new rate on the new money. A cash-out refinance replaces your whole mortgage at today’s rate, which can make sense when your current rate is already higher or you want one payment. I run both side by side.

How much can I borrow with a Texas home equity loan?

On a Texas homestead, your first mortgage plus the new loan can total up to 80% of the home’s value. Example: on a $600,000 home with $300,000 owed, 80% is $480,000, so up to about $180,000 before closing costs. Your credit, income and the appraisal set the final amount.

How long does a Texas home equity loan take?

Texas law requires at least 12 days between the required home equity notice and closing, and you have 3 days after closing to cancel before funds go out. Plan on roughly 3 to 5 weeks from start to cash, depending on the appraisal and documents.

How often can I take out a home equity loan in Texas?

Once every 12 months on your homestead. Texas allows only one home equity loan or cash-out refinance in any 12-month period, so it’s worth borrowing the right amount the first time.

What's the difference between a home equity loan and a HELOC?

A home equity loan pays a lump sum at a fixed rate with a fixed payment. A HELOC is a line of credit you draw from as needed, usually at a variable rate, so the payment can change. With today’s higher rates, the fixed payment of a home equity loan is easier to plan around.

Can I get a home equity loan on a rental property?

The Texas homestead rules apply only to the home you live in. For a rental, compare a DSCR cash-out refinance, which qualifies on the property’s rent, or an investment-property second loan where available.

Can I get a home equity loan if I'm self-employed?

Yes. My home equity programs can qualify you on 12 or 24 months of bank statements, 1099 income or a CPA-prepared P&L instead of tax returns, so your write-offs don’t shrink what you can borrow.

Can I get a home equity loan without income, using my assets?

Yes. Some of my home equity programs qualify you on eligible savings and investments instead of a paycheck, using asset depletion. It fits retirees, people between jobs and business owners after a sale.
