What should I know about this option?

How Do High-Net-Worth Borrowers Qualify Without W-2 Income?

Traditional retail banks underwrite mortgages using two years of personal and corporate tax returns. For high-net-worth borrowers whose wealth is held in brokerage accounts, private equity distributions, or business sale proceeds, traditional debt-to-income underwriting often fails to reflect true financial strength.

Through Asset Depletion Financing, wholesale underwriters convert your verified liquid assets (cash, publicly traded stocks, mutual funds, and vested retirement accounts) into qualifying monthly income by dividing the balance by 36, 60, or 84 months. Alternatively, certain programs use a coverage test verifying that net unencumbered assets equal the total loan balance plus 60 months of liabilities. Crucially, unlike private wealth banks that demand transferring millions in assets to secure relationship discounts, our wholesale programs simply verify statements—your assets remain completely unencumbered in your existing accounts.

Illustrative Case • Post-Exit Acquisition Austin (Lake Austin), Texas

Tech Founder Post-Exit — $3.2M Home Financed via Asset Depletion

The Situation: An Austin tech founder sold their software company for $8M, holding $3.5M in liquid brokerage accounts. They wanted to purchase a $3,200,000 waterfront home on Lake Austin with a $2,200,000 mortgage (69% LTV).

The Challenge: Having taken a sabbatical year after the sale, their current W-2 income was $0. A private bank offered a mortgage only on the condition that the founder transfer $3,000,000 in assets to the bank's wealth management division at a 1.25% annual management fee.

The Wholesale Solution: Adam structured a wholesale Asset Depletion jumbo loan. The lender discounted the $3.5M brokerage balance to $3,000,000 (85% haircut) and divided by 60 months, generating $50,000/month in qualifying income. The founder kept all assets at their existing brokerage, avoided tens of thousands in ongoing wealth management fees, and closed in 21 days with title vested in their revocable trust.

Disclosure: Illustrative scenario based on composite borrower profile. Actual interest rates, loan limits, and qualification guidelines depend on credit profile, property appraisal, and lender criteria.

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the questions Central Texas borrowers ask most.

How does the asset depletion divisor work?

Underwriters sum your eligible liquid assets (after applying standard haircuts such as 100% for cash, 80%–90% for publicly traded stocks, and 70% for retirement accounts if under age 59½) and divide the total by a set number of months (typically 36, 60, or 84). The result is added directly to your monthly qualifying income.

Do I have to transfer or pledge my brokerage assets?

No! On our wholesale asset depletion programs, underwriters only verify your account balances. You are never required to pledge your securities as collateral, move accounts, or pay ongoing wealth management fees to an affiliated bank.

Can I purchase a home in my Revocable Living Trust?

Yes. Most wholesale jumbo and asset depletion lenders permit title to be held in an eligible revocable living trust, provided the trust documents meet standard Texas title and underwriting criteria.

Can Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) count toward qualifying income?

Yes. If you work for a publicly traded company and have a documented history of receiving and vesting RSUs for at least two years, underwriters can average past vesting and project future vesting as allowable qualifying income.

What are the reserve requirements for asset-based luxury mortgages?

Depending on loan size and loan-to-value, lenders typically require 6 to 12 months of post-closing principal, interest, taxes, and insurance (PITIA) reserves remaining in your accounts. Because you already qualify on assets, these reserves are almost always easily satisfied.
