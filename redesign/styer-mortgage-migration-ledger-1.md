## Migration acceptance gates

1. Reconcile this 173-route ledger against repository/deployment output, Search Console landing URLs, analytics landing pages and server logs. Add missing historical routes before signing off “every existing page.”
2. Archive the current substantive body, headings, tables, FAQs, figures, source links, structured data and calculator behavior for each distinct page. This ledger identifies destinations but is not a byte-for-byte content backup.
3. Implement every required parent-to-child link in the ledger and a child-to-parent link. All substantive canonical pages require at least one relevant contextual inbound link beyond sitewide navigation.
4. Retain every established route. Ordinary content pages should serve their intended content successfully; keep existing application redirects and canonical aliases deliberate. No blanket redirects of old content to a homepage or generic hub.
5. Compare the pre/post content inventory by URL. Reordered or collapsed text must remain present; no lost examples, qualification explanations, case status, calculators or source references.
6. Crawl the proposed build from the homepage. Require zero missing ledger routes, zero broken internal links, zero substantive canonical pages without an inbound link, and no accidental noindex/robots/canonical changes.
7. Check mobile navigation, all eight standalone calculators, the scenario form, specialized construction screen, offer-review upload flow, guide delivery, call/text links, booking and secure application destinations.
8. Preserve appropriate page titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, author identity, dates and existing structured data. Validate that structured data matches visible content; do not invent reviews, outcomes or FAQ eligibility claims.

10. After launch, compare indexed URLs, errors, traffic and conversions against the prelaunch baseline. Preserving URLs and content reduces migration risk; it cannot guarantee unchanged rankings.
