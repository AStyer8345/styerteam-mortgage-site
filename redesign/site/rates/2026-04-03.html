This morning's jobs report was the headline — payrolls bounced back stronger than expected after February's dip. That hot number pushed rates up a few basis points. But here's the real takeaway: wage growth slowed. That matters to the Fed. It means the labor market is cooling on the wage side, which actually gives the central bank room to stay patient instead of rushing to hike rates.

Meanwhile, energy prices are still elevated thanks to international tensions, which keeps inflation a concern. The Fed isn't moving rates anytime soon — that's become pretty clear. For your buyers, that means if they're ready to move, locking in now gives them certainty instead of gambling on a bet that rates are coming down. We've seen too many buyers float and regret it.

Energy costs are real. Rate certainty is real. Let's talk about your client's number. Give me a call if you want to walk through what the updated rates mean for an active deal.
