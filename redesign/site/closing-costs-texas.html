Texas Closing Costs: What Buyers Actually Pay

Use the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure to separate loan charges, title and settlement costs, prepaids, escrow deposits, credits, and your down payment.

How Much Are Closing Costs in Texas?

Closing costs are not one universal percentage. On a Texas home purchase, your Loan Estimate may include lender charges, appraisal and other required services, title and escrow charges, recording fees, prepaid interest, homeowners insurance, and the initial escrow deposit. Your cash to close also includes the down payment, deposits, seller or lender credits, and other adjustments.

The exact amount depends on the price, loan, closing date, county, insurance, title coverage, and negotiated credits. Use your Loan Estimate—not a generic percentage—as the working budget. Then compare it with the Closing Disclosure you receive before closing and ask about every material difference.

What Appears on a Loan Estimate?

  • Loan costs: origination charges, points if selected, appraisal or credit services, and other services required for the loan.
  • Title and settlement costs: title-policy premiums, escrow or settlement services, recording-related work, and other transaction services.
  • Prepaids: daily interest, homeowners insurance, and property taxes or other charges due around closing.
  • Initial escrow payment: the amount collected to begin an escrow account for taxes, insurance, or other items when the loan requires one.
  • Credits and adjustments: deposits already paid, seller or lender credits, tax prorations, down payment, and other contract adjustments that change cash to close.

Compare estimates using the same loan amount, product, rate-lock status, points, closing date, and property assumptions. A lower rate may include more points, and a lender credit may offset upfront cost in exchange for a different rate.

What Is Different About Texas Title Costs?

Texas regulates title-insurance premium rates. The sale price and loan amount affect the policy calculation, and a simultaneously issued loan policy may receive the state-defined rate treatment. Escrow, recording, delivery, tax-certificate, and other settlement charges can still vary. Check the current Texas Department of Insurance title FAQs, the 2026 title-rate chart, and the actual title quote for the transaction.

Who Pays Which Closing Costs?

The purchase contract and loan structure decide which party pays each charge. Buyer costs commonly include loan charges, required services, prepaids, escrow deposits, and title or settlement charges. Seller-paid items and credits are negotiable and must comply with the selected loan program.

Do not rely on a customary-payer list as a promise. Ask the lender and settlement agent to show each charge, credit, and tax adjustment on the transaction documents.

How Can You Compare or Reduce Costs?

  • Compare complete Loan Estimates. Match the loan assumptions and review rate, points, lender charges, services, credits, and cash to close together.
  • Review services you can shop for. The Loan Estimate identifies services where the provider choice may affect cost.
  • Compare homeowners insurance early. Premiums affect both cash to close and the ongoing monthly payment.
  • Evaluate credits in context. A seller or lender credit can reduce upfront cash, but its contract terms, eligibility rules, price, points, and rate still matter.

How Should You Review the Closing Disclosure?

For most covered mortgages, the Closing Disclosure is due at least three business days before the scheduled closing. Compare the loan terms, lender charges, services, credits, prepaids, escrow deposits, and cash to close with the latest Loan Estimate.

Costs can change for different reasons. Ask the lender or settlement agent to explain every material difference instead of assuming that every fee is fixed.

Texas Closing Cost FAQ

How much are closing costs in Texas?

There is no single percentage that fits every Texas purchase. Your Loan Estimate may include lender charges, required services, title and settlement charges, recording fees, prepaid interest, homeowners insurance, and an initial escrow deposit. Cash to close also reflects the down payment, deposits, credits, and other adjustments.

Who pays closing costs in Texas?

The purchase contract and loan structure determine which party pays each charge. Buyer costs often include loan charges, required services, prepaids, escrow deposits, and title or settlement charges. Seller-paid items and credits are negotiable and must comply with the loan program.

Can seller credits cover buyer closing costs?

Seller credits may cover eligible buyer costs when the purchase contract and loan program allow them. The permitted amount and eligible charges depend on the loan, occupancy, down payment, sales price, and underwriting rules, so confirm the structure before relying on a credit.

Is title insurance required in Texas?

A lender may require a loan title policy. An owner's title policy is a separate coverage decision. Texas regulates title-insurance premium rates, while escrow, recording, delivery, tax-certificate, and other settlement charges can still vary.

How can I reduce closing costs when buying a home in Texas?

Compare Loan Estimates using the same loan assumptions, review which services you can shop for, compare homeowners insurance, and evaluate any seller or lender credit together with its effect on price, rate, points, and long-term cost.
