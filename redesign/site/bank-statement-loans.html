Who can a bank statement loan help?

Who can a bank statement loan help?

A bank-statement program may calculate income from 12 or 24 months of eligible deposits rather than tax-return income, subject to its requirements. Business deposits are reduced for operating expenses before they count. In these program examples, a standard expense factor is 50%, and a P&L or expense letter from an independent tax professional can lower it to a floor of 10% to 15% depending on the program. Other programs may use a schedule instead of the flat 50%: 15%, 30% or 50% for a service business with zero, one-to-five, or more than five employees; 25%, 50% or 80% to 85% for a product business. Some personal-statement methods count documented business-to-personal transfers at 100%; mixed accounts receive business-account treatment. That treatment must be confirmed, and the same income cannot be counted twice. Transfers between your own accounts, loan proceeds and refunds are excluded before any factor is applied. These program examples include primary-residence purchases up to 90% loan-to-value, cash-out examples up to 80%, loan limits of $3 million to $4 million, and minimum credit scores of 620 to 660 at reduced leverage. These limits come from different program conditions and cannot be assumed to apply together to one file.

What actually moves the file

The expense calculation can change the result. A lower factor needs documentation the selected lender accepts. For illustration, a consultant might be assessed at 15% rather than 50% under a program that accepts the supporting expense documentation; assuming $400,000 of eligible deposits over 12 months and 100% ownership, those expense factors produce about $28,333 versus $16,667 per month before any further program adjustments. This illustrates the calculation, not a qualifying loan amount. The example program requires the letter to come from the preparer who filed the return, address every expense category, and contain no accuracy disclaimer. Check the selected lender’s current requirements for who can prepare it and what it must say.

The account trail matters too. In one personal-statement method, eligible transfers count in full after two or three months of business statements document the source. A mixed account receives business-account expense treatment on eligible deposits. These are program-specific methods, not a reason to move money or count the same income twice. I sort the accounts and the expense evidence before I pick the program, because the lowest rate and the strongest income calculation may come from different programs.

A funded cash-out refinance for a homeowner with 1099 income used 12 months of personal bank statements and a CPA letter documenting business expenses of no more than 25%. The cash-out reimbursed home-repair spending and paid off renovation-related credit-card debt. The expense evidence and deposit review both mattered.

How do eligible deposits become qualifying income?

How do eligible deposits become qualifying income?

First identify the deposits the program treats as income. Then apply the required expense treatment, ownership share and other adjustments. Transfers between accounts, loan proceeds and other non-income deposits do not become earnings simply because they appear on a statement.

Illustration: $240,000 of eligible business deposits

Assume a lender accepts $240,000 of eligible receipts over 12 months, applies an assumed 50% expense factor, and the borrower owns 100% of the business.

  1. $240,000 ÷ 12 = $20,000 average monthly eligible deposits.
  2. $20,000 × (1 − 50% assumed expenses) = $10,000 after expenses.
  3. $10,000 × 100% assumed ownership = $10,000 monthly income in this illustration.

The $240,000 already excludes ineligible deposits. The expense factor and ownership calculation are assumptions, not universal program rules. Income trends and the complete file may change the result. This example does not establish a loan amount or approval.

Tax returns deserve the same careful review. A conventional calculation can include permitted add-backs, such as eligible depreciation, and other adjustments. Comparing unadjusted Schedule C profit with all bank deposits would misstate both methods. See Fannie Mae’s Schedule C guidance.

Should we review 12 or 24 months?

Should we review 12 or 24 months?

The appropriate lookback depends on the program and what your business history shows. A 12-month period captures a more recent year; 24 months can show a longer pattern or a full comparison between seasons. Neither period automatically produces a better result.

Bank statement loan questions

Bank statement loan questions

What is a bank statement loan?

A bank statement loan is a mortgage option that uses eligible personal or business deposits as the primary evidence for a self-employed income calculation. The lender reviews deposit sources, expenses, ownership and income trends alongside credit, debts, funds and the property.

Does every deposit count as income?

No. The review separates eligible income from transfers, borrowed funds, refunds, and deposits without a documented business source. Program examples include a 50% business expense factor, a supported lower factor, or a 15% to 85% schedule based on business type and headcount. Some programs require a written explanation when a deposit exceeds a month’s qualifying income. The selected program controls the calculation and documentation.

Will I still need tax returns or other records?

A selected bank-statement program may use eligible deposits as the primary income documentation. That does not guarantee a file will need no tax returns, transcripts or other records. Business verification, deposit explanations and additional documents can still be required for the specific file.

Can I use personal statements instead of business statements?

Some programs accept personal statements. One program method counts eligible business-to-personal transfers at 100% with no additional expense reduction, supported by two or three months of business statements. In these examples, mixed accounts receive business-account expense treatment; ownership requirements include 25% for a personal-statement method and 50% for a business-statement method. Confirm the current method and ownership requirement; the same earnings cannot be counted twice.

Does a 12-month program mean I only need one year in business?

No. Statement period and business history are separate requirements. These program examples require two years of self-employment; one calls for an additional 12 months of statements if the first 12 do not establish stability. Confirm the selected lender’s current history requirement.

Can a bank statement loan be used to buy or refinance?

Programs may allow purchases or refinances. These program examples include purchases up to 90% loan-to-value, cash-out examples up to 80%, and loan limits of $3 million to $4 million. Availability and combinations of these limits depend on current program requirements and the full file. For a rental property, also compare DSCR financing based on the property’s rental income.

Will it cost more than a conventional mortgage?

It may. Different documentation can carry different pricing and down-payment requirements. If your tax returns support conventional financing, compare it with the bank-statement option. I compare available offers using the same assumptions and pricing date, including rate, points, fees, payment, and cash required.

I'm a 1099 contractor. Can I qualify on my deposits?

Depending on your file, we can compare 12 or 24 months of eligible deposits with a 1099-only program where available. One 1099 program example uses gross 1099 income with a 10% expense factor. Compare the actual eligibility, expense treatment, and supported income under each method; additional deposits are not automatically qualifying income.

What if I co-mingle business and personal accounts?

Mixed accounts do not automatically rule out a review. In these program examples, a mixed personal/business account receives business-account treatment, with the selected expense factor applied to eligible deposits; one standard factor is 50%. Confirm the lender’s method for your accounts. Separate accounts can make the source and movement of money easier to document. The same earnings cannot be counted twice; personal-account treatment still depends on the selected program.

What's the difference between 12 and 24 month programs?

A 12-month review uses a more recent year; 24 months can show two business cycles and seasonal patterns. The lender evaluates trends and sustainability. Neither period is automatically better, and the statement period is separate from the required time in business.

What credit score do I need?

These program examples have minimum credit scores of 620 to 660 at reduced leverage. The selected program may require a higher score for the loan amount, loan-to-value, or property. Stronger credit may improve available terms, but it does not establish eligibility by itself.

How much down payment?

These program examples allow primary-residence purchases with as little as 10% down (90% loan-to-value) and cash-out refinances up to 80% loan-to-value. Lower credit, larger loans, property details, and other program conditions may require more equity. Confirm the current requirement for your file.

How long does closing take?

Timing depends on the income review, the complete file, appraisal, and title. I identify the expected documents early, and Tiffany coordinates questions and updates. We can discuss a realistic timeline once we know the file; a one-day pre-approval should not be assumed.

More about the statement review

More about the statement review

Personal accounts, business accounts and duplicate deposits

Identify which accounts receive customer payments and which receive transfers. If business receipts move to a personal account, the review must avoid counting the same income twice. The program determines how business expenses and ownership are documented for each account type.

When a P&L or expense statement is relevant

Some program examples accept an expense statement from an independent CPA, enrolled agent, or CTEC preparer, with expense floors of 10% to 15%. In the method discussed here, the statement must come from the preparer who filed the returns, cover all expenses, and contain no accuracy disclaimer; wording such as “approximately” or “to the best of my knowledge” may be unacceptable under that method. The selected lender decides which preparers and statements it accepts. Do not assume a letter replaces its full income review.

Records that can help explain the file

After the initial conversation, the selected path may require complete statements for the review period, proof of business ownership and activity, explanations for unusual deposits, expense information and records for closing funds or reserves. Additional documents depend on the file. Share financial records through the secure process Adam provides.
