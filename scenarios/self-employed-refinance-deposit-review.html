A Self-Employed Refinance: Which Deposits Count as Income?

By Adam Styer, NMLS #513013 · September 13, 2026

A large deposit can change a bank-statement mortgage review, but the lender needs to understand what the payment represents. In this refinance file, the work centered on a disputed business payment and the expenses used to calculate qualifying income. Final acceptance was still unresolved in the review described here.

The situation

A self-employed homeowner wanted to review refinancing. The income came through business activity, including a substantial payment reported as part of the borrower’s annual 1099 compensation. A bank-statement approach was being evaluated to document the income available to support the new mortgage.

What made it challenging

The underwriter excluded a payment described as a bonus. Removing it reduced the income used for qualification and increased the ratio of monthly debt payments to income. There was a second question: did the expense assumption used in the calculation accurately reflect the business?

The approach considered

Explain the payment

The proposed response connected the deposit to the borrower’s ongoing work and requested supporting payer and compensation records. Reporting a payment on a 1099 was relevant evidence; it did not, by itself, settle whether the lender would accept it in this calculation.

Document the expenses

The next step was to obtain a CPA’s review of the business expenses. The percentage needed to come from actual records, with the period and business activity clearly identified. A more favorable percentage could not simply be selected to make the loan work.

Recalculate the complete file

The review needed to show the effect of the accepted income on the borrower’s debts and proposed payment. The disputed deposit and expense treatment both mattered; resolving only one might not be enough.

Where things stood

This was an underwriting documentation issue, with a response being prepared and additional support requested. The available review does not establish that the payment was accepted, that an expense adjustment was approved, or that the refinance closed.

What you can take from this: Before assuming your business deposits support a particular loan amount, separate the money received from the income a lender can use. A useful review explains both the deposits and the cost of earning them.

What to bring to a similar conversation

  • The statements for the period the lender requests
  • An explanation and supporting records for unusual business payments
  • Business ownership information and an expense breakdown from your records
