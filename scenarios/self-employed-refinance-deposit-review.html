Self-employed · Funded cash-out refinance

1099 Income, Bank Statements: A Funded Cash-Out Refinance

By Adam Styer, NMLS #513013 · Updated October 9, 2026

A self-employed homeowner with 1099 income completed a cash-out refinance using a 12-month personal bank-statement program. A CPA letter documented business expenses of no more than 25%. The cash-out reimbursed money spent on home repairs and paid off credit-card debt accumulated during the renovation.

Borrower
Self-employed homeowner
Goal
Reimburse repairs and pay off renovation debt
Qualification method
12 months of personal bank statements

Based on a real funded loan. Identifying details are omitted. This outcome applies to this file, not to every borrower or program.

The situation

A self-employed homeowner had paid substantial out-of-pocket costs to repair major damage to the home. Credit-card balances had also accumulated during the renovation. The refinance goal was to reimburse those repair expenditures and pay off the related card debt. The borrower received 1099 income, but the selected qualification method used bank statements.

The funded outcome

The refinance funded through a 12-month personal bank-statement program. The cash-out reimbursed the borrower for renovation spending and paid off the credit cards accumulated during the repairs. The file moved from an underwriting documentation review to a completed refinance.

What you can take from this: Before assuming your business deposits support a particular loan amount, separate the money received from the income a lender can use. A useful review explains both the deposits and the cost of earning them.

How the review worked: details and questions

What made it challenging

The underwriter excluded a payment described as a bonus. Removing it reduced the income used for qualification and increased the ratio of monthly debt payments to income. There was a second question: did the expense assumption used in the calculation accurately reflect the business?

The documentation that moved the file

Explain the payment

The review connected the deposit to the borrower’s ongoing work with supporting payer and compensation records. Reporting a payment on a 1099 was relevant evidence; it did not, by itself, settle whether the lender would accept it in this calculation.

Document the expenses

A CPA letter documented that the borrower’s business expenses were no more than 25%. That support addressed the expense question in this file. The percentage came from the business evidence; it is not an expense factor another borrower can automatically use. The letter did not replace the bank-statement review or the rest of underwriting.

Recalculate the complete file

The income review was considered alongside the mortgage payment, existing debts, available equity and the proposed debt payoff. A cash-out refinance also changes the mortgage balance, payment and costs; the amount available to the borrower must be reviewed after payoffs and closing costs.

What to bring to a similar conversation

  • The statements for the period the lender requests
  • An explanation and supporting records for unusual business payments
  • Business ownership information and an expense breakdown from your records
