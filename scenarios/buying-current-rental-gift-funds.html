Buying the Home You Rent: A Better Plan for Gift Funds

By Adam Styer, NMLS #513013 · September 13, 2026

For these renters, the decision was how to use family help to make homeownership workable. The discussion compared putting more money down with a smaller down payment and targeted debt payoff, subject to program rules. The goal was to understand the total monthly obligation and cash needed, rather than focus only on the mortgage payment.

Based on a real consultation or file review. Identifying details are omitted. The status below reflects this stage of the financing process.

The situation

The borrowers wanted to purchase the home they were already renting directly from their landlord. Family gift funds were available, and the seller had some flexibility on timing. A preliminary purchase proposal existed, but the transaction still needed a completed financing review and an executed contract.

What made it challenging

The available funds had several possible jobs: down payment, closing costs, and potentially paying off existing debts where permitted. Putting more money toward the home could reduce the mortgage payment, while paying off a high-payment debt could change the household’s total obligations. The borrowers also wanted to inspect unresolved property issues before finalizing the purchase.

The approach considered

Compare two uses of the available funds

The call explored a larger down payment alongside a smaller down payment with selected debt payoff. The comparison needed to show the mortgage payment, remaining debt payments, mortgage insurance where applicable, and cash left after closing.

Build the complete monthly budget

The requested payment target needed to be tested against taxes, insurance, and any association dues as well as the loan payment. Eliminating a separate debt payment could affect the household budget differently from reducing the mortgage balance.

Keep the purchase and documentation moving together

The next steps included reviewing the purchase proposal for financing requirements, documenting gift funds, and completing the loan application. Inspection findings could inform further discussions with the seller, while any repair or credit arrangement still needed to fit the selected loan.

Where things stood

The documented result was a purchase-planning approach and a request to finalize the contract and application. The review did not establish a final loan structure, a confirmed payment, or a completed closing.

What you can take from this: A bigger down payment is one option to compare. Ask to see what each use of the funds does to your total monthly obligations, cash to close, and savings after the purchase. Confirm allowable gift and debt-payoff treatment before moving money.

What to bring to a similar conversation

  • The purchase proposal or signed contract, if available
  • The gift amount and donor relationship
  • Current debt payments and the monthly budget you want to maintain
