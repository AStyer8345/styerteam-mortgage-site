# Appraisal and home inspection

An appraisal is an independent opinion of value prepared for the lending process and may also address program property requirements. A home inspection is a separate evaluation chosen by the buyer to understand the property's condition. An appraisal is not a substitute for an inspection.

# Low appraisal or appraised value below the purchase price

When an appraisal is below the contract price, the loan calculation may be limited by the lower value under the program. Possible next steps can include reviewing the report for factual errors, a lender-managed reconsideration process, renegotiation, additional borrower funds, a different loan structure, or contract remedies. The available option depends on the facts and contract; the assistant must not predict an appraisal or give contract advice.

# Primary residence, second home, and investment property

Occupancy affects eligible loan programs, pricing, down payment, reserves, and documentation. A primary residence is the home the borrower intends to occupy as required by the loan. A second home and an investment property have separate program definitions. Occupancy must be stated truthfully; misrepresenting intended occupancy can constitute mortgage fraud.

# Condominiums

Condominium financing may involve review of both the unit and the project. Insurance, budgets, reserves, owner occupancy, litigation, structural or safety issues, commercial space, assessments, and project eligibility can matter. Approval of one unit or a prior loan does not establish that a new transaction will be eligible.

# Two- to four-unit homes

Residential mortgage programs may finance eligible two- to four-unit properties. Owner-occupied transactions may have different down-payment, reserve, rental-income, self-sufficiency, and appraisal requirements than one-unit homes. Five or more units are generally treated as commercial or multifamily rather than standard one- to four-family residential lending.

# Manufactured homes

Manufactured-home eligibility depends on the home, land, title, foundation, construction standards, age, modifications, property classification, appraisal, and program. A mobile-home description alone is not enough to determine financeability.

# New construction and construction loans

Construction financing may be a construction-only loan followed by permanent financing or a single-closing construction-to-permanent structure. Builder approval, plans, specifications, budget, draw administration, inspections, contingency funds, land equity, completion, and conversion terms vary by program. A builder's incentive or advertised rate should be compared with the full price and loan costs.

# Homeowners, flood, wind, and title insurance

Lenders generally require acceptable property insurance. Flood insurance may be required when the property securing the loan is in a designated special flood hazard area, and other coverage may be required by location or property type. Lender's title insurance protects the lender's covered title interest; owner's title insurance is a separate product. Coverage and legal questions belong with the insurer, title company, or attorney.
