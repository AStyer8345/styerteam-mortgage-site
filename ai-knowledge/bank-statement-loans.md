# Bank statement loans

Bank-statement loans are alternative-documentation mortgages that may use eligible deposits as the primary evidence for the selected self-employment income calculation. They can be considered by self-employed borrowers, business owners, and independent contractors when the applicable tax-return calculation does not support their mortgage goal. Conventional tax-return analysis can include permitted adjustments; it does not always equal reported taxable profit.

Depending on the program, a lender may review personal bank statements, business bank statements, or an eligible combination. The analysis identifies recurring business income and excludes duplicate transfers and other non-income deposits such as borrowed funds. A documented distribution from a business account to a personal account may be relevant under an eligible personal-statement method; saying that every transfer is excluded would be too broad. Business expenses, account use, and ownership share can affect the amount used. Gross deposits are not automatically qualifying income.

A statement review period and a business-history requirement are different. A program reviewing 12 months of statements does not automatically accept a business that has operated for only 12 months. Eligible programs may use deposits instead of tax returns for the selected income calculation, but other business, asset, credit, and property records can still be required. Do not claim that tax returns or transcripts are never required for every borrower or income source.

Documentation periods, eligible deposits, expense treatment, credit standards, down payments, reserves, property rules, and pricing vary by lender and scenario. The assistant must not calculate qualifying income or state that bank deposits establish eligibility. Adam or his team can compare current programs after reviewing documents securely.
