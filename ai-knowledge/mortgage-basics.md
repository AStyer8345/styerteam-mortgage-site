# What is a mortgage?

A mortgage is a loan secured by real property. The borrower repays principal and interest under the note, and the security instrument gives the lender rights in the property if the loan is not repaid as agreed. Loan terms, costs, and protections depend on the product and transaction.

# What is included in a mortgage payment?

A monthly housing payment may include principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, flood insurance, and other required items. Condominium or homeowners-association dues are usually paid separately but are commonly considered when qualifying. The exact payment must come from a current loan estimate or reviewed scenario.

# Fixed-rate and adjustable-rate mortgages

With a fixed-rate mortgage, the note rate does not change during the loan term, although the total payment can change when taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, or escrow requirements change. An adjustable-rate mortgage has an initial rate period followed by adjustments based on the note's index, margin, adjustment schedule, and caps. The initial ARM rate should not be treated as the permanent payment.

# Interest rate and APR

The interest rate is the percentage used to calculate interest on the loan balance. Annual percentage rate, or APR, is a disclosure measure that incorporates the interest rate and certain finance charges. APR can help compare similar offers, but it is not the note rate and does not include every possible ownership cost.

# Discount points and lender credits

One discount point equals 1% of the loan amount. Paying discount points generally means paying more at closing for a lower interest rate. A lender credit generally reduces upfront closing costs in exchange for a higher rate. The value of either choice depends on the exact pricing and how long the borrower expects to keep the loan; the assistant must not recommend a choice or calculate a break-even result from chat data.

# Rate locks

A rate lock is a lender commitment to hold specified pricing for a stated period subject to the lock terms and unchanged application details. Lock periods expire, extensions may cost money, and pricing may change if the loan or borrower information changes. A rate lock is different from a prequalification or approval.

# Loan term and amortization

The loan term is the scheduled repayment period. Amortization is the process of reducing principal through scheduled payments. A shorter term often produces a higher required principal-and-interest payment and less total interest than a longer term at the same balance and rate, but actual pricing and costs vary.

# Prequalification and preapproval

These terms are not used identically by every lender. A prequalification may be based on preliminary or unverified information. A preapproval generally involves a more complete application and review, but it is still conditional and is not a final loan approval or assurance of closing. Property, documentation, credit, program, and underwriting conditions remain.
