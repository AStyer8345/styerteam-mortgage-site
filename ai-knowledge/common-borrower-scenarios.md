# Do I need 20% down?

No. Some eligible conventional programs allow as little as 3% down, FHA commonly permits a 3.5% minimum required investment at qualifying scores, and VA may require no down payment when entitlement and transaction requirements are satisfied. The actual minimum depends on the borrower, property, occupancy, loan amount, program, and lender.

# Can I buy before selling my current home?

Possible approaches may include qualifying with both properties, using eligible sale proceeds, a bridge loan, a home-equity product, recasting after sale, or another buy-before-you-sell structure. Each creates different lien, payment, timing, liquidity, and sale risks and requires a complete review.

# Can I use rental income to qualify?

Rental income may be considered when supported by the applicable lease, tax returns, appraisal rent schedule, property history, or other required documentation. Vacancy factors, expenses, ownership history, occupancy, and program rules affect the amount used. Proposed rent is not always usable dollar for dollar.

# Can I qualify after bankruptcy or foreclosure?

Mortgage programs have different waiting periods and requirements after bankruptcy, foreclosure, deed-in-lieu, or short sale. The event type, discharge or completion date, property disposition, re-established credit, extenuating circumstances, and new loan program matter. The assistant must not calculate an eligibility date without document review.

# Can I get a mortgage with collections or charge-offs?

Collections or charge-offs do not produce one universal answer. The program, automated findings, balance, type of account, dispute status, legal exposure, required payoff, and overall credit profile matter. Paying an account can also change the reported credit information, so a borrower should obtain scenario-specific guidance before acting solely to qualify.

# Can gift money cover the down payment?

Many programs permit eligible gifts, but donor eligibility, documentation, transfer, borrower contribution, reserves, and permitted uses vary. The funds should not be transferred or documented casually; the lender should provide the current requirements.

# Can I change jobs during the mortgage process?

A job change does not automatically end a mortgage application, but it can require reverification and may change how income is analyzed. Moving from salary to commission, W-2 employment to self-employment, or a permanent role to contract work can materially affect the review. Tell the lender before making or accepting a change.

# Can I make a large purchase before closing?

New debt, reduced assets, new credit inquiries, or changed payments can affect underwriting and cash to close. Borrowers should consult the loan team before financing a vehicle, furniture, appliances, or other large purchases and should continue making all obligations on time.

# Why did my payment change?

Even with a fixed note rate, a total payment can change because of property-tax assessments, insurance premiums, flood coverage, mortgage insurance, escrow analysis, shortages, or other serviced items. A variable-rate loan may also change under its adjustment terms. Servicing disputes require review of the statement and escrow analysis by the servicer.

# What should I avoid sending in chat?

Do not send Social Security numbers, full birth dates, account or card numbers, passwords, authentication codes, identification documents, tax returns, bank statements, pay stubs, or other sensitive records. Use the approved secure application or another approved secure channel.
