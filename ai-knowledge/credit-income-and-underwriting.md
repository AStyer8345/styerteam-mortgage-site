# Credit scores and credit history

Mortgage review can consider credit scores, payment history, recent inquiries, account balances, utilization, collections, judgments, bankruptcies, foreclosures, disputed accounts, and the depth and age of credit. A score is only one part of the analysis. Different scoring models, report dates, borrowers, and lender rules can produce different results.

The assistant may explain general program baselines found in approved sources, but it must not predict how an action will change a score, provide credit-repair advice, or tell a visitor that a score qualifies them.

# Hard and soft credit inquiries

A hard inquiry is generally associated with an application for credit and may affect a score. A soft inquiry is generally used for screening or account review and normally does not affect a score in the same way. The lender should explain what type of inquiry will be used and obtain any required authorization.

# Debt-to-income ratio

Debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares qualifying monthly debt obligations with qualifying gross monthly income. The calculation may include the proposed housing expense, installment and revolving debts, support obligations, leases, other mortgages, and additional required liabilities. Which debts and income count, and the acceptable result, depend on the program and underwriting findings. The assistant must not calculate DTI or state a maximum for an individual from chat information.

For a general conventional-loan explanation, there is not one universal maximum across every conventional loan. Under Fannie Mae's current published guidance, a file receiving an eligible Desktop Underwriter result may have a total DTI as high as 50%. A manually underwritten Fannie Mae file generally has a 36% maximum, which can reach 45% when the published credit-score and reserve requirements are met. Freddie Mac's published guidance generally makes a mortgage ineligible when monthly DTI exceeds 45%. Program, automated-underwriting, loan-purpose, and lender-overlay rules can produce a lower limit. The assistant may explain these general ceilings but must not say that any ratio establishes eligibility.

When a visitor asks what their DTI “needs to be,” explain that lower generally gives the file more room, but there is no single target that ensures an approval decision. Answer the general question first, then ask only for the one scenario detail that would most improve the next answer.

# Employment and income stability

Underwriters generally evaluate whether income is eligible, documented, stable, and reasonably expected to continue under the applicable program. Salary, hourly, overtime, bonus, commission, self-employment, retirement, Social Security, disability, alimony, child support, rental, and other income types can have different history and documentation rules. A recent job change does not automatically prevent financing, but the details require review.

# Variable income

Overtime, bonus, commission, seasonal, gig, and other variable income commonly require a documented history and an analysis of consistency and likelihood of continuance. An average may be adjusted when income is declining or irregular. The assistant must not average income or decide whether it is usable.

# Student loans

Student-loan obligations may be included in qualifying even when payments are deferred or reported as zero. The payment used depends on the loan program, documentation, repayment status, and current agency or lender rules. Income-driven repayment, forgiveness plans, deferment, and forbearance require individual review.

# Co-borrowers and non-occupant co-borrowers

A co-borrower shares responsibility for the debt and is evaluated under the applicable program. Some programs permit an eligible non-occupant co-borrower, but occupancy, relationship, down-payment, property, and underwriting rules can differ. Adding a co-borrower does not by itself establish approval.

# Automated and manual underwriting

Many loans use an automated underwriting system that evaluates the complete application against program rules and risk factors. Some files may require or permit manual underwriting. Findings can change when credit, income, assets, debts, property, loan terms, or documentation changes.
