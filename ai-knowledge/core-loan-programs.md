# FHA loans

FHA loans are government-insured mortgages offered through approved lenders. They are commonly used for a primary-residence purchase and may be useful for borrowers seeking a lower down payment or more flexible credit guidelines than some conventional options.

Under FHA's baseline guidelines, a minimum decision credit score of 580 is associated with the 3.5% minimum required investment. Scores from 500 through 579 require at least a 10% minimum required investment. Those are FHA program baselines, not a promise that every lender will approve a loan at those scores. Lenders and investors may impose higher minimum scores or other overlays, and approval depends on the full application, credit history, income, debts, funds, property, and automated or manual underwriting findings.

FHA loans require mortgage insurance and generally require the property to be the borrower's principal residence. Eligible property types may include one- to four-unit homes when program occupancy and property requirements are satisfied. FHA appraisal and minimum property standards apply. Gift funds and eligible assistance may be allowed subject to sourcing, documentation, and program rules.

# Conventional loans

Conventional mortgages are not insured by FHA or backed by VA. Conforming conventional loans generally follow Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac eligibility and underwriting rules, while lenders may also apply their own requirements.

Some conventional purchase programs permit down payments as low as 3%, but the available minimum depends on the program, occupancy, property type, income limits, first-time-buyer status, and underwriting result. A 20% down payment is not universally required. Private mortgage insurance is generally required when the loan-to-value ratio is above the applicable threshold, and its cost and cancellation rules depend on the loan and law.

There is no single credit score that ensures conventional approval. Automated underwriting evaluates the overall application, and current agency rules, lender overlays, loan purpose, property type, down payment, reserves, debts, and credit history can affect the result. Adam or his team must review an individual scenario before discussing eligibility.

For a useful general answer, a score around 620 is a common starting point for many conforming conventional programs, not a universal promise or a definition of “good.” Higher scores can improve pricing or mortgage-insurance options, while the full credit profile and automated-underwriting result still matter. The assistant should present 620 as a common reference point rather than saying the visitor qualifies.

# VA loans

VA-backed home loans are available to eligible veterans, service members, and certain surviving spouses who obtain the required Certificate of Eligibility and meet applicable VA and lender requirements. VA does not set a universal minimum credit score, although lenders may set their own minimums and evaluate the complete credit profile.

When asked for a “good” VA score, lead with that direct distinction: VA itself has no universal minimum. A stronger score can still improve lender choice or pricing, and each lender may set a minimum. Ask about the visitor's goal or timing only after answering the score question.

VA does not generally require a down payment when the borrower has sufficient entitlement and the transaction meets program and lender requirements. A down payment may still be required in some cases, including when remaining entitlement is limited or the price exceeds the appraised value. VA loans do not require monthly private mortgage insurance, but a VA funding fee may apply unless the borrower is exempt. Occupancy, residual-income, property, appraisal, entitlement, and lender underwriting requirements apply.

# Jumbo loans

Jumbo loans exceed the applicable conforming loan limit or otherwise fall outside standard conforming execution. They are not one uniform program. Credit standards, down payment, reserves, eligible income, property types, loan sizes, and pricing vary by lender and investor.

Jumbo files often receive closer review of assets, reserves, income stability, credit history, and property characteristics. The assistant may explain the concept but must not state a minimum score, required down payment, maximum loan amount, or likely approval without a current program and scenario review.

# Down payment assistance

Down payment assistance may be offered through state or local housing agencies, nonprofits, employers, or other approved programs. Assistance can take different forms, including grants, forgivable subordinate loans, deferred-payment loans, or repayable second liens. It is not always free money, and repayment, occupancy, income, purchase-price, property, homebuyer-education, or first-time-buyer conditions may apply.

In Texas, programs may be available through organizations such as TSAHC or TDHCA and may be paired with eligible FHA, VA, or conventional first mortgages. Program terms, participating lenders, funding, rates, and eligibility can change. Adam or his team must verify the current program and the borrower's scenario before describing available assistance.

# Credit-score and eligibility questions

When asked for a program's minimum credit score, distinguish the government or agency baseline from a lender's current minimum and from an approval decision. A credit score alone does not determine eligibility. The assistant may explain an approved baseline, but it must not tell a visitor that they qualify, predict approval, or request detailed credit-report information in chat.
