How do you qualify when you work for yourself?

Start by matching your income records to an eligible mortgage program. A lender reviews the required history, the income or assets available to support repayment, your debts and funds for closing, and the property. The document list depends on the path being considered.

Tax returns may support the loan, or an eligible alternative-documentation program may use bank statements, 1099s, a P&L or assets. Being self-employed does not by itself decide which program fits.

Which documents tell your income story?

These are starting points for a review, not universal checklists. Conventional, FHA, VA and jumbo options may still work when the borrower and property meet the relevant requirements.

Tax returns and business records

The review considers the required personal and business returns, applicable schedules, ownership and the income that can support the mortgage. Business earnings, losses and distributions can require additional analysis.

Why deposits, revenue and qualifying income are different.

Illustrative example — no lender guideline or approval implied. A contractor’s business account receives customer payments, a transfer from another account and borrowed funds. The total deposits are not all business earnings.

A deposit-based review first identifies eligible receipts. It then applies the selected program’s business-expense treatment and other required adjustments. A tax-return analysis uses its own rules for eligible income and adjustments. The two calculations can produce different results.

Neither method means every deposit counts or every deduction can be added back. The useful number is the documented qualifying income under the actual program being considered.

What to prepare for a useful first review.

Start with the context

  • The purchase, refinance or other financing goal and your timing.
  • Your business type, ownership share and time in business.
  • How you are paid: customer receipts, 1099s, wages, distributions or other income.
  • Any seasonal pattern, recent income change, new entity or lender concern.

Know which records are available

  • Personal and business tax returns, where relevant to the selected path.
  • The required bank statements, 1099s, P&L and business-verification records.
  • Statements for funds intended for closing and reserves.
  • Information on existing debts and the property being financed.

You do not need every item for the initial conversation. The lender’s current checklist controls the formal file. Send sensitive records only through the appropriate secure process.

If you plan to use business money for closing, mention it early. Access to the funds and the effect on business operations can require review. If your income recently changed or the business is new, flag that rather than assuming a standard history rule applies to every program.

Self-Employed Mortgage FAQ — Austin TX

Self-employed borrowers may be able to use conventional, government-backed, jumbo, bank-statement, 1099, P&L, asset-depletion, or DSCR documentation depending on the borrower, property, and current program. Some alternative-documentation programs may not rely primarily on tax returns; eligibility and calculations require lender review.

A bank statement program may use eligible deposits from personal or business statements to calculate qualifying income. Statement periods, excluded deposits, expense treatment, documentation, and eligibility vary by lender and current program.

Requirements vary by lender and current program. The review may include self-employment history, credit, down payment or equity, reserves, bank statements, business documentation, deposit analysis, and property eligibility. Tax returns may not be the primary income documents on some programs.

Business deductions can reduce the income available for a tax-return-based mortgage calculation. Depending on the borrower and current program, an eligible bank statement, 1099, P&L, or asset-based path may use a different documented calculation.

Credit requirements and pricing vary by lender, program, loan-to-value, occupancy, property, documentation, and the rest of the borrower profile. Review a current written matrix and quote for the specific scenario.

Down payment, eligible sources of funds, reserves, and maximum loan-to-value vary by lender, program, occupancy, property, credit, loan amount, and documentation. For market context, the Austin–Round Rock–San Marcos median sales price was $435,000 in July 2026, but the required cash to close must be calculated for the specific property and program.

These programs offer different ways to document self-employed income. A bank statement program may review deposit history; a 1099 program may use eligible 1099 income; and a P&L program may review a qualifying profit-and-loss statement. Documentation periods, expense treatment, eligibility, and pricing vary by lender and program.

Required self-employment history varies by agency or investor program. Prior work in the same field, current business history, income stability, credit, down payment, reserves, and supporting documents may affect eligibility. Review the complete current requirements before relying on a shorter history.
