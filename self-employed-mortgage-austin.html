What should I know about this option?

Can I get a mortgage when I work for myself?

Yes. Working for yourself does not, by itself, block a mortgage in Austin. A conventional, FHA, VA, or jumbo loan above the conforming limit may fit when the records support the payment. If you own 25% or more of a business, many agency loans ask for two years of tax returns. Some expenses, such as depreciation, can be added back. Not every deduction can.

My team and I compare the tax-return calculation with bank deposits, 1099s, a CPA profit and loss statement, and eligible assets. Gross deposits are not qualifying income, and not every deduction is added back. Looking at the file does not require you to amend your taxes.

Clear Answers

Common questions

Do I need a special mortgage because I am self-employed?

No. Conventional, FHA, VA, and jumbo loans above the conforming limit may be available when your income, credit, funds, and property meet that program. An alternative-documentation mortgage is worth comparing when a different income calculation solves a specific problem.

Do I need to change my tax deductions before buying a home in Austin?

Start with an honest review of the records you already have. Some deductions can be added back, and another documentation method may fit. Talk tax planning through with your tax preparer. A mortgage review should not assume that giving up a legitimate deduction is your only option.

Can I qualify with less than two years of self-employment?

Some programs allow a shorter history in specific cases. How old the business is, the related work you did before, the earnings you can document, and whether the business can continue all matter. A 12-month bank-statement option does not automatically mean 12 months in business is enough.

Can I use business funds for the down payment or closing costs?

Possibly. The lender has to see that you can reach the funds, and it may need to look at what taking the money out does to the business. Tell me early if the same business will provide both the income and the money for closing.

Can K-1 income help me qualify?

It can. A K-1 reports income from an entity. It is not its own loan type, and it is not automatic proof that the cash is available to you. Ownership, distributions, the strength of the business, and your access to the income decide the analysis. See how K-1 income is reviewed.

What credit score and down payment do I need?

It depends on the path. On bank statement loans, minimum credit starts around 620 to 660, and the strongest files can buy with 10% down, which is 90% loan-to-value. Conventional, FHA, and VA follow their own minimums when your tax returns support the loan.

Can self-employed borrowers get a mortgage in Austin, TX?

Yes. Self-employed borrowers in Austin can qualify for conventional, FHA, VA, a jumbo loan above the conforming limit, or an eligible alternative-documentation mortgage. The path depends on the income you can document, credit, funds, and the property. Being self-employed is not, by itself, a reason for a no.

What is a bank statement loan and how does it work?

It uses 12 or 24 months of deposits instead of tax-return income. Business deposits are reduced by an expense factor, often 50% to start, or as low as 10% to 15% with a qualified expense letter from your tax preparer. Transfers, loan proceeds, and refunds come out first. The deposits are not qualifying income until that review is done. More detail is on the bank statement loan page.

What do I need to qualify for a self-employed mortgage in Austin?

Start with the property goal, the type of business, your ownership, how long you have been at it, and how you get paid. The lender then verifies the income method it will accept, credit, debts, down payment or equity, and reserves. Tax returns, statements, 1099s, or a P&L may be needed, depending on the program.

Why do write-offs affect my mortgage qualification?

Deductions can reduce the profit on the tax return, but the income a mortgage uses is not always that bottom line. Conventional rules allow some add-backs, and another documented method may fit. Compare both calculations before you assume the write-offs block the loan.

What is the difference between a bank statement loan, a 1099 loan, and a P&L loan?

Three ways to document the same business. A bank statement loan averages 12 or 24 months of deposits, then subtracts an expense factor. A 1099 loan starts from gross 1099 income with a flat expense factor. A P&L loan uses a profit-and-loss statement your CPA prepares. I run the ones that fit, because the one that qualifies you for the most is rarely the one with the better price.
