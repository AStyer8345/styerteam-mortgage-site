Can You Get a Mortgage If You're Self-Employed in Austin?

Self-employed borrowers may be able to use conventional, government-backed, jumbo, bank-statement, 1099, P&L, asset-depletion, or DSCR documentation depending on the borrower, property, lender, and current program. Some alternative-documentation programs may not rely primarily on tax returns; eligibility, documentation, income calculations, pricing, and reserves require lender review.

Business deductions can reduce the income available for a tax-return-based calculation. That does not automatically rule out a mortgage, but it makes an early comparison of eligible documentation paths important.

The practical first step is to compare how each available path treats income, deposits, business expenses, assets, reserves, occupancy, and the property before choosing a program.

This is not a loophole. Bank statement and 1099 programs sit outside the Qualified Mortgage safe harbor, but they are still governed by the federal Ability-to-Repay rule. The CFPB's ATR/QM rule at 12 CFR §1026.43 requires a lender to make a reasonable, good-faith determination that you can repay — using verified income or assets. It does not require a tax return specifically. Your bank statements, your 1099s, your CPA's P&L: those are the verification. That's the legal reason these programs exist, and why a properly built non-QM file is not a no-doc loan.

Program availability changes. Bank statement, 1099, P&L, and other alternative-documentation options must be checked against current lender guidelines and pricing for the borrower, property, occupancy, and transaction.

Self-Employed Mortgage Programs Available in Austin

There's no single "self-employed mortgage." There are several programs — each built for a different documentation situation. The right one depends on how you track income.

Bank Statement Loans

A bank-statement program may calculate income from eligible deposits after excluding transfers and other non-income items and applying the program's expense treatment. Statement period, supporting documents, credit, leverage, reserves, and pricing vary by investor.

Compare when: Eligible deposits may document the business cash flow more clearly than a tax-return calculation.

1099 Income Loans

A 1099 program may use eligible 1099 income with a program-specific expense treatment. Required history, tax or business documents, supporting deposits, credit, leverage, reserves, and pricing vary by investor. See the full 1099-only mortgage guide for Texas.

Compare when: Eligible 1099 income is the clearest documented income source.

P&L Loans

A P&L program may use an eligible profit-and-loss statement and supporting documentation to calculate income. Preparer qualifications, coverage period, bank-statement review, expense treatment, add-backs, credit, leverage, reserves, and loan limits vary by investor. See how a P&L mortgage in Texas is reviewed.

Compare when: An eligible P&L and supporting records may document current business income.

Asset Depletion / Asset Utilization

Asset-depletion programs may calculate qualifying income from eligible documented assets after required deductions. Agency and investor-specific eligibility, asset discounts, divisors, leverage, reserves, rates, and documentation differ.

Compare when: Eligible documented assets may support the file more effectively than recurring earned income. Run an illustrative calculation →

What You'll Need to Qualify

Requirements vary by lender and program. A current review should cover self-employment history, credit, down payment or equity, reserves, income documentation, deposit treatment, loan amount, occupancy, and property eligibility.

Self-Employment History

Required history and acceptable evidence of self-employment vary by lender and program. Confirm the documentation before relying on a business license, professional letter, bank statements, or prior employment in the same field.

Credit Score

Credit requirements and pricing vary by lender, program, loan-to-value, occupancy, property, documentation, and the rest of the borrower profile.

Down Payment

Down payment, eligible sources of funds, reserves, and maximum loan-to-value vary by lender, program, occupancy, property, credit, loan amount, and documentation.

Bank Statement Income

The lender identifies eligible deposits, excludes transfers and other non-income items, and applies the program's expense treatment. Statement period, expense methodology, and supporting business documentation vary by program.

Reserves

Reserve amount, eligible account types, required ownership, and any asset discounts vary by lender and program. Confirm what must remain after closing for the specific transaction.

Documents Required

The lender's checklist controls. It may include identity, personal or business bank statements, business verification, deposit sourcing, property documents, and other items needed for the selected program.

Why Tax-Return and Alternative Calculations Can Differ

Eligible business expenses can reduce the income available for a tax-return-based calculation. A conventional analysis reviews the complete required business and personal tax documentation under current agency rules.

For example, a consulting business may have gross revenue that is materially higher than the net income shown after eligible business expenses on Schedule C of Form 1040. A conventional analysis and an eligible alternative-documentation analysis can therefore produce different qualifying-income results.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide addresses ownership and the required self-employed income analysis. The specific calculation depends on the income source, ownership, documentation, history, and business condition.

On an eligible bank statement path, the lender reviews the required statement period, identifies qualifying deposits, excludes transfers or other non-income items, and applies the program's expense methodology. The result must be calculated under the selected lender's current written guidelines.

This isn't creative accounting. It's using the right loan program for the way you actually operate your business.

Pricing can differ between conventional and alternative-documentation programs and can change with the market. Compare same-day written quotes using the same borrower, property, loan amount, occupancy, term, lock period, points or credits, and closing-cost assumptions.

Quick Answers for Self-Employed Borrowers

What is the best mortgage option for a self-employed borrower in Austin?

The best option depends on which documentation tells the cleanest income story. Some self-employed borrowers qualify conventionally with tax returns, while others need bank statements, 1099 income, a CPA-prepared P&L, asset depletion, or DSCR if the property is an investment.

Do business write-offs automatically prevent mortgage approval?

No. Write-offs can reduce qualifying income on a tax-return file, but they do not end the conversation. Adam compares the tax-return path against deposit-based, 1099-based, P&L, and asset-based documentation before deciding which route is realistic.

Can a self-employed borrower still use conventional, FHA, VA, or jumbo financing?

Yes. Standard loan programs can still work when the documented income, credit, assets, and property fit the guidelines. The complex-income pages focus on harder scenarios, but the review starts by checking whether a cleaner agency or jumbo path is available.

When should a borrower talk to Adam?

Start before making an offer or restructuring business documentation. A short scenario review can identify which documents matter, which income path may work, and whether the borrower should avoid a full application until the file is better prepared.
