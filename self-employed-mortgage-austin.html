What should I know about this option?

Can I get a mortgage when I work for myself?

Yes. Being self-employed does not disqualify you from buying a home or refinancing in Austin. However, traditional retail banks underwrite self-employed borrowers through a rigid guideline: if you own 25% or more of a business, they require two full years of personal and business tax returns, qualifying you solely on net taxable income after all legal write-offs, depreciation, and business deductions.

As an independent wholesale mortgage broker, Adam Styer provides alternative documentation loan programs that evaluate real operating cash flow. Using 12 or 24 months of business or personal bank deposits, 1099 revenue, or CPA-prepared P&L statements, you can qualify for the home you actually afford without amending your tax returns or paying unnecessary taxes to the IRS.

1. Conventional Schedule C Add-Back Formula (Original Baseline)

Assume a sole proprietor reports $72,000 in annual net profit and $12,000 of depreciation (permitted Fannie Mae add-back under Selling Guide B3-3.2):

($72,000 Net Profit + $12,000 Depreciation) ÷ 12 = $7,000 per month qualifying income

2. Bank-Statement Deposit Calculation ($240,000 Baseline Receipts)

Assume $240,000 of eligible business deposits over 12 months ($20,000/mo) with a 50% assumed expense factor and 100% ownership:

$240,000 ÷ 12 = $20,000/mo × (1 − 50% assumed expenses) = $10,000 monthly qualifying income ($120,000/year)

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical, straightforward answers to the questions self-employed borrowers ask most in Austin.

Do I need a special mortgage because I’m self-employed?

No. Many self-employed borrowers qualify for standard conventional, FHA, VA, or jumbo loans using tax returns with allowable add-backs like depreciation. When write-offs make taxable income too low, bank statement, 1099, or P&L loans provide alternative qualification paths without tax returns.

Do I need to change my tax deductions before buying a home?

Not necessarily. Before paying higher taxes just to qualify for a conventional loan, compare the cost against a bank statement or P&L mortgage. In many cases, paying the slightly different rate on a non-QM loan costs substantially less than the extra income taxes you would pay by skipping legitimate business deductions.

Can I qualify with less than two years of self-employment?

Yes. If you have at least 12 months of self-employment history in the same line of work or industry where you previously held employment, several wholesale portfolio programs can approve the loan with strong credit and reserves.

Can I use business funds for the down payment or closing costs?

Yes. Business funds can be used for down payment, closing costs, and reserves as long as you own the required percentage of the business (usually 25–100%) and the withdrawal does not negatively impact business operations.

Can K-1 income help me qualify?

Yes. If you receive K-1 distributions from an S-Corporation, LLC, or partnership, we can count either your ordinary business income (if you own 25%+) or documented cash distributions paid to you.

What credit score and down payment do I need?

For bank statement programs, down payments start at 10% on strong files, with 15–20% being common. Credit scores start around 620 to 660, with the best wholesale pricing at 720+.

What is a bank statement loan and how does it work?

A bank statement loan qualifies you based on your true cash flow rather than taxable income on IRS returns. We review 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank statements, total eligible deposits, apply a business expense factor (typically 10–50%), and use that figure as your qualifying monthly income.
