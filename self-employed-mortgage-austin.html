Self-Employed Mortgage in Austin, TX

Working for yourself changes how you document income. Compare conventional loans and alternative paths based on your tax returns, deposits, or other business records.

Can I get a mortgage when I work for myself?

Yes. Self-employment does not automatically require a special loan. The first question is how much stable, usable income your records support. A conventional, FHA, VA or jumbo mortgage may fit; bank statements, 1099s, a profit-and-loss statement or eligible assets may offer another way to document income.

The best path depends on how you earn, what the records show and the loan’s total cost. Strong business revenue is helpful context, but it is not the same as the income available for your mortgage payment.

Why can tax returns and deposits produce different answers?

A tax-return calculation does not always stop at the net-profit line. Under the applicable rules, it can add back certain items, such as eligible depreciation, and remove income that is not recurring. A deposit calculation starts with eligible receipts, then addresses business expenses and ownership. Neither method simply uses your gross revenue.

A simple tax-return illustration

Assume a sole proprietor reports $72,000 in annual net profit and has $12,000 of depreciation that qualifies for an add-back, with no other adjustments in this simplified example.

($72,000 + $12,000) ÷ 12 = $7,000 per month before any further history, trend or program analysis.

This illustration shows why net profit and qualifying income may differ. It is not a loan approval or an estimate of how much you can borrow. The complete file may require other adjustments.

The next useful comparison is the documented deposit calculation, not the account’s total inflow. See a bank-statement example with expense and ownership assumptions.

Does one of these situations sound like yours?

These are illustrations of what changes the decision, not completed loan results.

Established owner with significant write-offs

Start with the adjusted tax-return calculation. If it falls short, compare eligible deposits after expenses. The decision turns on the usable income each method supports and the cost of each available loan.

Contractor with uneven monthly payments

Review the full earnings pattern and the source of large payments. A high month does not establish a sustainable average; 1099s, statements and business records may explain the pattern together.

Business owner with cash tied up in the company

Separate qualification from cash to close. Even when income works, withdrawing money for a down payment may affect operations. Compare the financing plan with the liquidity the business needs to keep.

For questions about preserving business cash, ownership changes and purchase timing, see mortgage planning for business owners.

What if my business is new or income has changed?

The period a lender reviews is different from the age of your business. A program using 12 months of bank statements may still require a longer self-employment history. Changing entity type, adding an owner or moving from employment to contracting can also change what needs to be documented.

Some conventional cases can use less than two years of self-employment when the latest signed returns show a full year in the current business and prior related earnings at the same or a higher level support the history. Experience and business debt also need review. Fannie Mae explains those conditions; alternative-documentation lenders set their own requirements.

If income recently rose or fell, start with when it changed, why it changed and whether the new level is likely to continue. A lender may request current records rather than rely on an older average alone.

What are the tradeoffs?

Payment and total cost

An alternative calculation can help a file qualify, but it may carry different rates and fees. Compare actual offers and the payment you can comfortably sustain.

Cash needed and cash kept

Down payment, closing costs and required reserves are separate pieces. Consider what you want available for the business and your household after closing.

Documents and timing

Fewer tax documents does not mean no documentation. Unclear deposits, ownership changes or incomplete records can affect the review and transaction timing.

The goal is a supported, affordable choice. If your tax returns already qualify you on suitable terms, that deserves a fair comparison. A smaller purchase or more preparation time may also fit your plans better.

Self-employed mortgage questions

Do I need a special mortgage because I’m self-employed?

No. Conventional, FHA, VA and jumbo options may be available when your income, credit, funds and property meet the program’s requirements. An alternative-documentation mortgage is worth comparing when a different supported income calculation addresses a specific obstacle.

Do I need to change my tax deductions before buying a home?

Start with an accurate mortgage-income review of your existing records. Some deductions receive permitted adjustments, and another documentation method may fit. Discuss tax-planning decisions with your tax professional; a mortgage review should not assume that giving up legitimate deductions is your only option.

Can I qualify with less than two years of self-employment?

Some programs allow shorter histories under specific conditions. Business age, your prior related work, documented earnings and the business’s ability to continue all matter. A 12-month bank-statement option does not automatically mean 12 months in business is enough.

Can I use business funds for the down payment or closing costs?

Possibly. The lender must establish that you can access the funds and may need to evaluate how withdrawing them affects the business. Tell Adam early if the same business will provide both your qualifying income and your money for closing.

Can K-1 income help me qualify?

Potentially. A K-1 reports income from an entity; it is not a separate loan type or automatic proof of cash available to you. Ownership, distributions, business strength and access to income determine the analysis. See how K-1 income is reviewed.

What credit score and down payment do I need?

There is no single minimum for every self-employed borrower. The answer depends on the mortgage program, credit, loan amount, property use, income evidence and funds remaining after closing. Compare the payment and cash needed for the actual options your file supports.

Compare the income paths that fit your business

Start with your financing goal, business type, ownership share, time in business and how you get paid. Include any recent change or deadline. Estimates are enough for the first conversation.
