Start with the property, not a Dallas-wide payment estimate.

A Dallas home search can involve different counties, taxing units and homeowner associations. Two homes with the same purchase price can have different monthly costs. Start with the actual address, then compare principal and interest, property taxes, insurance, HOA dues and any applicable mortgage insurance or assessments.

For a Dallas County property, use the Dallas Central Appraisal District tax resources and estimator. If your search expands toward Plano, Frisco or another North Texas community, confirm the county before selecting an appraisal district; DCAD links to neighboring appraisal districts. Ask for a property-specific estimate instead of assuming a seller's existing tax bill will be your future bill.

A useful comparison for your next two showings

Bring the addresses, asking prices, down-payment budget, HOA information and insurance estimates. Compare total housing expense and cash needed at closing on the same loan assumptions. A lower asking price alone does not identify the more affordable property.

When your tax return does not tell the whole story.

Dallas business owners, consultants and buyers with uneven income can start by comparing the conventional documentation path with available alternatives. A business deposit is not automatically qualifying income: ownership, business expenses, transfers and the lender's documentation rules still matter.

Self-employed or business income

Review the business history and how income is documented before choosing a program. Keep operating cash needs separate from the funds intended for your home purchase.

Investments or retirement assets

Identify account types, accessible funds and the money needed for down payment and reserves. An asset-based calculation is a starting point for review, not an approval.

Explore asset-based qualification →

For a larger purchase or a combination of salary, investments and business proceeds, compare the high-net-worth planning guide and jumbo financing options. Program availability and terms depend on the complete borrower and property file.

Buying or refinancing a Dallas rental?

Keep the property's financing analysis separate from your household mortgage. Review the rent evidence, existing lease, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, purchase price or value, and any liens being paid off. The DSCR calculator helps compare rent with the modeled housing payment; it does not account for every operating expense or establish lender eligibility.

For cash-out, specify the payoff, transaction costs and cash you want to receive. The new loan amount is not the same as spendable proceeds. Read the Texas DSCR financing guide for the existing statewide program discussion, then review the property-specific assumptions with Adam.

Compare written options on the same assumptions.

For a home purchase or refinance, compare the rate, points or lender credits, lock period, total payment and cash to close together. The CFPB's Loan Estimate explainer shows where these costs appear. Use the mortgage-offer review if you already have a written quote, or start with the payment calculator if you are still setting a budget.

Dallas mortgage questions

Do you serve Dallas if your office is in Austin?

Yes. Adam Styer is based in Austin and serves borrowers across Texas, including Dallas. This page describes service coverage; it does not represent a Dallas branch office.

Can a Dallas buyer use assets instead of salary to qualify?

An asset-based option may be worth reviewing when eligible assets are substantial and documented income is limited. Account type, access to funds, down payment, reserves and the lender’s rules determine the calculation and eligibility.

Which tax estimate should I use for a Dallas-area home?

Use the actual property address and its appraisal district and taxing units. Confirm insurance and HOA costs separately. A metro-wide tax percentage or the seller’s current bill may not reflect your future housing expense.

Do you also help first-time buyers and conventional borrowers in Dallas?

Yes. The review can include traditional home-purchase and refinance options as well as complex-income scenarios. Share your purchase timeline, down-payment budget and income type so Adam can identify the next documents to review.

Talk through your Dallas plans.

Start with your timeline, intended property use, income type and an approximate purchase or refinance amount. Adam can identify what needs a closer review and where to begin.
