How DSCR Cash-Out Refinancing Differs in Texas

This calculator is for a rental that is outside your Texas homestead. Under Texas Constitution Article XVI, §50(a)(6), a home equity loan on a homestead, together with other liens on that homestead, is limited to 80% of fair market value.

For a non-homestead rental, the lender sets the loan-to-value limit and evaluates rent, payment, property and loan requirements. If you’re financing your homestead or are unsure about its status, start with the Texas home equity guide.

How the estimate works

At selected LTV, new loan = property value × LTV. At target cash, new loan = (payoffs + other costs + penalty + desired cash) ÷ (1 − percentage charge). Net cash subtracts those payoffs and all modeled costs from the new loan.

PITIA combines principal and interest with entered taxes, insurance and HOA. Coverage is entered rent ÷ PITIA. The comparison uses the same property costs for both loans; it does not model mortgage insurance, retained subordinate debt, future rate changes, balloons or lifetime interest over different remaining terms. A lower modeled payment is not proof of savings.

Required reserves are separate from transaction expenses: some displayed cash may need to remain available. Later escrow refunds and future penalties on the new loan are excluded. Actual costs, qualifying rent, value, terms and availability require a lender review.

Portfolio Equity Harvest • North Austin, TX (Travis County)

Extracting $160,000 to Fund Two New Out-of-State Rentals

An Austin investor owned a single-family rental in North Austin purchased in 2017. The home appraised at $520,000 with a remaining mortgage balance of $210,000. Market rent was $3,200/month.

By structuring a Texas DSCR cash-out refinance at 70% LTV ($364,000 new loan amount), the investor paid off the $210,000 existing mortgage and extracted approximately $145,000 in net cash proceeds after closing costs.

Compare the full housing payment, closing costs, and loan terms using a current written quote. Because gross rent was $3,200/month, the property achieved a comfortable 1.24x DSCR ($3,200 / $2,580). The investor deployed the $145,000 as 20% down payments on two additional rental units.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About DSCR Financing

Direct answers to the most frequent underwriting, reserve, and vesting questions.

Are DSCR cash-out refinances subject to Texas Section 50(a)(6) limits?

Section 50(a)(6) sets limits for home equity loans secured by a Texas homestead. The lender sets the loan-to-value limit and title requirements for a non-homestead rental.

How much cash can I pull out with a Texas DSCR refinance?

Start with the estimated new loan and subtract the liens it will pay off to get gross cash out. Subtract closing costs and any additional penalty to estimate what you’d receive. Available loan amounts depend on the property and lender.

What seasoning period is required before pulling cash out?

Lenders can require you to own the property for a minimum period before using current appraised value. If you recently paid cash, ask whether the lender offers delayed financing and how it sets the loan amount.

Can I get cash-out on a property that is vacant?

Some lenders can use the appraiser’s market rent on a vacant property. Others require a lease or rental history. I’ll check the lender’s rules for the property.

What can I use the cash-out proceeds for?

Because DSCR loans are business-purpose commercial loans, proceeds are typically intended for business, real estate acquisition, property improvement, or liquidity purposes.
