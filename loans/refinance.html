What Are Your Refinance Options in Texas?

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new loan featuring updated terms, interest rates, or balances. In Texas, refinances fall into two primary categories: Rate-and-Term Refinancing (adjusting your rate or shortening your term without extracting cash) and Texas Section 50(a)(6) Cash-Out Refinancing.

Under the Texas Constitution, cash-out refinances are capped at 80% Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV) to safeguard homeowner equity. Texas law also mandates a mandatory 12-day cooling-off disclosure period, requires closings to take place at a licensed title company or attorney's office, and limits closing fees to 2% of the loan amount.

Illustrative Case • Rate & PMI Elimination Austin (Circle C), Texas

Circle C Homeowner — Dropping PMI & Saving $462/Month

The Situation: An Austin couple bought a Circle C home three years ago for $580,000 with 5% down ($551,000 initial loan), paying $175/month in private mortgage insurance (PMI). Current loan balance was $525,000.

The Appraisal & Opportunity: Recent neighborhood comparable sales showed the home was now worth $710,000, bringing their current loan-to-value to 74% (26% equity).

The Wholesale Refinance Solution: Adam locked a new conventional 30-year fixed mortgage with a wholesale lender at a 0.625% lower interest rate. Because the LTV was well under 80%, PMI was eliminated completely. Combined principal, interest, and PMI savings totaled $462/month ($5,544/year). Total closing costs were recovered in just 11 months.

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the questions Central Texas borrowers ask most.

How do I calculate my refinance break-even point?

Divide your total closing costs by your monthly payment savings. For example, if closing costs are $3,600 and you save $300 per month, your break-even point is 12 months ($3,600 / $300). If you plan to stay in the home longer than 12 months, refinancing is financially advantageous.

What is the Texas 50(a)(6) cash-out rule?

Section 50(a)(6) of the Texas Constitution dictates that total mortgage debt (including first mortgage and any cash taken out) cannot exceed 80% of the home's fair market value. It also includes a 12-day cooling-off disclosure period and limits lender closing fees to 2%.

Can I refinance to remove PMI without putting down extra cash?

Yes! If your home has appreciated since purchase and your current loan balance represents 80% or less of the current appraised value, refinancing into a new conventional mortgage eliminates monthly PMI immediately.

How soon can I refinance after purchasing a home?

For a rate-and-term refinance, there is typically no waiting period for conventional loans (or 6 months for certain lender guidelines). For a Texas cash-out refinance, you must have owned the property for at least 6 months.

Can closing costs be rolled into the refinance loan?

Yes. On rate-and-term conventional, FHA, and VA refinances, standard closing costs and prepaid property tax and insurance escrows can typically be rolled into the new loan balance so you bring minimal or zero cash to closing.
