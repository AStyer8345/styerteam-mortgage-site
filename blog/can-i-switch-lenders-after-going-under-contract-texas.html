Can you switch mortgage lenders after going under contract in Texas?

By Adam Styer | NMLS #513013 | Published April 12, 2026 | Updated November 15, 2026

There is no universal cutoff date for switching mortgage lenders before closing. The new lender must independently approve the file, decide whether it can accept the existing appraisal, clear the remaining conditions, and deliver required disclosures in time for the scheduled closing.

I get this question a lot — usually from buyers who went with their realtor's recommendation or the builder's preferred lender, got the Loan Estimate, and realized the numbers don't look right. A switch may be possible before closing, but the file and contract timeline have to support it.

Can You Switch Lenders After Going Under Contract?

Generally, yes—before closing. A new lender still has to complete its own application, underwriting, appraisal review, title review, conditions, and disclosures. Changing lenders does not automatically move the financing or closing deadlines in your purchase contract.

Before making the change, review the purchase contract and financing addenda with your real estate agent or attorney. Ask the proposed lender to confirm in writing that its milestone schedule fits the contract date.

One exception worth noting: if you're buying a new build and signed an incentive agreement tied to using the builder's preferred lender (rate buydown, closing cost credit, etc.), switching lenders may reduce or eliminate those incentives. Compare both written scenarios before deciding.

When Does It Make Sense to Switch?

Not every reason to switch is a good reason. Here are the situations where it genuinely makes sense:

  • You got a better rate or lower fees. This is the most common reason. Compare written quotes for the same borrower, property, and loan structure; pricing varies by program and market conditions. Mortgage pricing depends on the borrower, property, loan structure, and market conditions.
  • Your current lender is dragging their feet. If they're slow to respond, keep asking for the same documents, or can't give you a clear timeline — that's a red flag. Slow lenders blow up closing dates. Switching early is better than scrambling at the end.
  • The builder's preferred lender isn't competitive. Builder lenders often quote rates that include their incentive package, which makes the numbers look comparable on paper. But once you strip out the buydown or credit, the underlying rate and fees may be higher than what you'd get on the open market. Upload your Loan Estimate for a free comparison — that's the fastest way to find out.
  • Your situation changed. Maybe your credit score improved since you first applied. Maybe rates dropped. Maybe you realized you want a different loan product — ARM instead of fixed, or conventional instead of FHA. A new lender can re-price based on your current profile.

What's the Timeline for Switching Lenders in Texas?

Count backward from the contract closing date using confirmed milestones, not a universal 14-day, 21-day, or replacement-appraisal rule. The relevant questions are whether the appraisal can be accepted, whether the file is complete, how quickly underwriting and conditions can be cleared, and when the Closing Disclosure can be delivered.

An existing appraisal may be usable, but transfer is not automatic across every loan or lender. FHA uses a formal case and appraisal transfer process. For conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae, a new lender may accept another lender's appraisal only when appraisal-independence and Selling Guide requirements are satisfied. Ask the new lender to review the appraisal before assuming it can be used or budgeting time for a replacement.

For most covered mortgages, the borrower must receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Some loan types use different disclosures, so the lender should identify the exact requirement for your transaction.

Before moving the file, ask the proposed lender for a written milestone schedule covering application, appraisal review, underwriting, remaining conditions, Closing Disclosure delivery, and the contract closing date. A short timeline may work, but it is lender- and file-specific rather than guaranteed.

Will Switching Delay My Closing?

It can. The risk depends on the work the new lender must repeat, whether it accepts the appraisal, the completeness of the file, unresolved title or insurance items, and the time needed for required disclosures.

Do not treat an extension as assured. If the written milestone schedule does not fit the contract date, discuss the risk and any extension request with your real estate agent before switching.

What Do I Need to Switch?

The same documents you already provided to your original lender:

  • Most recent 30 days of pay stubs
  • W-2s for the past 2 years
  • Federal tax returns for the past 2 years (if self-employed or commission-based)
  • Bank statements for the past 2 months
  • Government-issued photo ID
  • Your executed purchase contract

Plus one thing you didn't need the first time: your current Loan Estimate. This is the document that shows your interest rate, closing costs, and monthly payment from your existing lender. A new lender needs to see it to give you a real apples-to-apples comparison — not a vague quote, but a concrete side-by-side.

If you've already gathered everything for Lender A, switching to Lender B is mostly paperwork logistics. The heavy lifting is done.

How Does a Mortgage Broker Make Switching Easier?

When you go direct to a bank or a retail lender, switching means starting completely over with a brand new company. New application, new account, new loan officer learning your file from scratch.

With a mortgage broker, more than one wholesale lender may be available through the same point of contact. A change still depends on the receiving lender's requirements, and the borrower may need updated disclosures, documents, or another application.

If you're coming from a bank, builder lender, or online lender, send the current Loan Estimate and contract date. I can compare the written terms and identify the milestones that would have to be completed before a switch makes sense.
