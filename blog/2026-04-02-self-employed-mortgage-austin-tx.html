To prepare for a self-employed mortgage, gather the records that show your business income: personal and business tax returns, a current profit and loss statement when requested, business bank statements, and proof of self-employment such as a business license or CPA letter. Lenders look at qualifying income after permitted adjustments, not just your net profit, so ask how your deductions are treated before you assume they rule you out. Depending on your records, a conventional loan or an alternative-documentation option such as a bank statement, 1099-only, or P&L loan may be worth comparing.

Preparing for a self-employed mortgage starts with understanding what your records can support. This article focuses on income history, business changes, documents, and funds for closing. For the main comparison of qualification methods, see self-employed mortgage options in Austin and across Texas.

You do not need to choose a loan program or change your tax strategy before the first conversation. Start with your property goal, how the business earns, and anything that recently changed. Then identify the records needed for a useful comparison.

What Do Lenders Look At for Self-Employed Borrowers?

A lender needs to establish stable qualifying income and understand how the business supports it. Depending on the program and file, useful records may include:

  • Personal and business tax returns — for the period required by the selected program
  • A current P&L statement — when needed to explain current earnings and trends
  • Business bank statements — for the requested period, with transfers and unusual deposits explained
  • Proof of self-employment — business license, CPA letter, or business registration

A tax-return review evaluates sustainable earnings under the applicable rules, including permitted adjustments and income trends. It does not always stop at the net-profit line or use a simple two-year average. The required history and number of returns depend on the file and program.

How Do Business Deductions Affect Qualifying Income?

Business expenses can reduce taxable income, but qualifying income can differ after permitted adjustments. For example, eligible depreciation may be added back under Fannie Mae’s Schedule C guidance (opens in a new tab). Other expenses still affect the income available for the mortgage.

Suppose a consulting business has $200,000 of revenue and reports $60,000 of Schedule C net profit. Neither figure alone establishes qualifying income. Review the return for permitted adjustments, then evaluate income history and current performance. If deposits are also considered, exclude non-income deposits and apply the selected program’s expense and ownership treatment.

Ask your mortgage professional to explain the adjusted income calculation before assuming your deductions prevent qualification. Your tax professional can address tax decisions; an alternative income method still needs evidence that meets its own rules.

What Are My Loan Options as a Self-Employed Borrower?

You have more options than most people realize.

Conventional loan — review adjusted business income

When adjusted, documented business income supports the payment, a conventional mortgage deserves consideration. Prepare the required returns and current business information, then compare eligibility, payment, fees, and funds needed with any other suitable option.

Bank Statement Loan

A bank-statement program may use eligible personal or business deposits as the primary income documentation. The lender excludes non-income deposits and applies its expense, ownership, and history requirements. The usable amount is not simply the account’s gross inflow.

Compare the actual rate, fees, payment, down payment, and reserves of a bank statement loan with other available options. A higher calculated income does not automatically make a higher-cost loan a good choice.

1099-Only Loan

For independent contractors, some programs use eligible 1099 earnings with required expense adjustments and supporting records. The document period and business-history requirements must both be checked. See 1099 mortgage options in Texas for a closer look.

P&L Only Loan

A P&L mortgage option may use a qualifying profit-and-loss statement with lender-required preparation and verification. Supporting statements or other records may still be needed. A P&L does not remove the need to confirm acceptable business history.

What Documents Do I Need for a Self-Employed Mortgage?

For a conventional review, gather the records requested for your file:

  • Complete personal tax returns for the required period
  • Complete business returns and ownership schedules when required
  • Current P&L and balance sheet if requested
  • Business statements for the requested period
  • Personal account records for closing funds and reserves
  • Business license or CPA letter confirming ownership and duration

For a bank statement loan:

  • Complete statements for the program’s review period, which may be 12 or 24 months
  • Business license or CPA letter
  • Expense or P&L records that meet the selected program’s requirements
  • Records showing the required down payment, closing costs, and reserves

How Should I Prepare Before Applying for a Mortgage?

The earlier you plan, the more options you have. A few things worth doing before you start the process:

  • Coordinate the review early. Have the mortgage professional calculate usable income from your existing records, and discuss any planned tax or entity changes with your CPA.
  • Keep a clear record of deposits. Retain invoices and transfer records that explain large payments, seasonal changes, and movement between accounts.
  • Discuss new borrowing before you apply. A new payment, guarantee, or business obligation can change the mortgage analysis. Explain the proposed debt before relying on an earlier estimate.
  • Know your gross revenue and your net. Bring both numbers to your first conversation with a lender. The gap between them is where we figure out which program works.

How I Work With Self-Employed Buyers

The first conversation can identify which records would help compare your options. A documented pre-qualification review is a later step and still depends on the required application, credit, income, asset, and property information.

I’ll compare conventional and suitable alternative-documentation options based on the records your file supports. When statements and returns are needed, I’ll arrange secure collection rather than asking you to send them through a public form or ordinary email.
