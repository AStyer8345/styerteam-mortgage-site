How Much Should You Put Down on a House?

What changes from 3% to 30% down, and what the next pricing tier is worth to you.

A
Adam Styer
Senior Loan Officer, NMLS #513013 · November 15, 2026

When I run a down payment comparison, the first thing I check is whether the extra cash gets you into a better pricing tier. Then I look at what you’d save each month and what you’d still have in the bank. You need both numbers to make the call.

What’s the minimum down payment on a conventional loan?

Eligible first-time buyers can put as little as 3% down on certain conventional loans for a one-unit primary home. If you’ve owned a home before, you may still qualify for 3% down through Fannie Mae HomeReady or Freddie Mac Home Possible if you meet the income and other program requirements. For most repeat buyers who don’t qualify for a 3% program, the starting point is 5% down. Fannie Mae’s 3% down guidelines and Freddie Mac Home Possible.

Where the down payment changes your rate and PMI

Start with 3% if you qualify, then compare 5%, 10%, 15% and 20%. If you have more cash available, check 25% and 30% too. You can put down an amount in between. These are the points where I’d check for a change in pricing or mortgage insurance.

Loan-to-value (LTV) is the share of the home’s value you’re borrowing. A bigger down payment means a smaller loan and more equity from day one. That gives the lender a bigger cushion if the home loses value and there’s a foreclosure. Lower risk can mean a lower rate or fewer points at the same rate. Below 20% down, mortgage insurance also affects the cost, so compare the full payment and cash to close.

Say you’re at 15% down and thinking about stretching to 17%. On a $650,000 home, that’s another $13,000 at closing. It shrinks the loan, but 85% and 83% LTV sit in the same base Fannie Mae purchase pricing tier. You may get the same rate and you’ll typically still have PMI. At 20% down, you hit a new tier and drop PMI on a conventional loan at 80% LTV.

Above 20%, I’d compare quotes at 25% and 30%. You’re already past the PMI cutoff, so the question is what better pricing and a smaller loan are worth to you. For the 740 credit score in this example, Fannie Mae’s base pricing adjustment improves at both thresholds. On your quote, that could show up as a lower rate or fewer points at the same rate. I compare quotes from the same day so we can see what the extra cash actually gets you. Fannie Mae purchase pricing matrix.

An example: 15% vs. 20% down on a $650,000 home

Both options use a 740 credit score and a 30-year fixed conventional loan. I’ve used a slightly higher rate at 15% down to show how the comparison works. Your lender could quote the same rate at both down payments.

The tradeoff. At 20% down, you bring $32,500 more to closing and save $305.81 a month at first. The smaller loan and the assumed rate account for $259.77 of that. PMI accounts for the other $46.04.

Which choice would give you more peace of mind?

Say you have a baby on the way, steady income and room in the monthly budget. The higher payment fits, and having that $32,500 in the bank would help you sleep better. In your shoes, I’d probably put 15% down. You can always pay extra toward principal later, once you know what the new expenses look like. That can also get you to the point where you can request PMI cancellation sooner. CFPB guide to PMI cancellation.

If you tell me the extra $306 every month would drive you crazy, and you’d still have a comfortable emergency fund after closing, I’d probably put 20% down. I ask buyers what they want left in the bank after closing and what they’re comfortable paying every month. Those two numbers tell me which option I’d choose in their shoes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a conventional mortgage with 3% down?

Yes, if you qualify for a 3%-down conventional program. Eligible first-time buyers have options for a one-unit primary home. Income-qualified repeat buyers may also be eligible through Fannie Mae HomeReady or Freddie Mac Home Possible. For most repeat buyers without a 3% option, the conventional minimum is 5%.

Will going from 15% to 17% down get me a better mortgage rate?

Usually, I wouldn’t expect a better rate just from that change. You’re moving from 85% to 83% LTV, which is within the same base Fannie Mae pricing tier. You’ll have a smaller loan, and you should still budget for PMI. Have your lender quote both options because rates, points and PMI can vary.

Does 20% down eliminate PMI?

Yes. On a conventional purchase, you don’t pay PMI when your loan is 80% or less of the home’s value. The lender uses the lower of the purchase price and appraised value to calculate that percentage. If the appraisal comes in low, have your lender rerun the numbers.

Can 25% or 30% down get me a better mortgage rate?

Yes, it can. Putting 25% down gets you to 75% LTV, and 30% down gets you to 70% LTV. Those are separate conventional pricing tiers. Compare the rate at the same upfront cost, or compare points and credits at the same rate.

Can I pay extra principal to get rid of PMI sooner?

Yes. On eligible loans, extra principal payments can get your balance to 80% of the home’s original value sooner. Once you reach that point, you can ask your servicer in writing to cancel PMI. You’ll generally need to be current, have a good payment history, and meet the requirements for property value and other liens.

If you’re weighing two down payments, I can run them side by side. You can also use the mortgage payment calculator to try different amounts.
