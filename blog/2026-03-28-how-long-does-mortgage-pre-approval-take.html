Here's exactly what drives the timeline and how to make sure you're not the bottleneck.

The Short Answer: Once the Documents Are In

A W-2 employee with straightforward income and a clean credit profile can get a pre-qualification letter once the documents are in. The loan officer reviews your documents, runs your application through an automated underwriting system (Fannie Mae's DU or Freddie Mac's LP), and issues the letter once the system returns an approval.

The rest of the work is collecting the documents.

Where it slows down:

  • You're self-employed. Two years of personal and business tax returns, a year-to-date P&L, and sometimes a CPA letter. More documents = more review time. Once those documents are in, we review them and issue a pre-qualification letter.
  • Your income is complicated. Commission, bonus, rental income, multiple jobs — the underwriting system needs to calculate a qualifying income from 24 months of history.
  • Recent credit events. A bankruptcy, foreclosure, or collections account that needs explanation adds time — not because the lender is slow, but because they need the full story before they can commit.
  • Missing documents. This is the #1 cause of delay. Going back and forth for a missing bank statement or second W-2 is what holds the letter up.

What You Need to Have Ready

Have these before you apply and you won't be the bottleneck:

W-2 Employees

  • Last 2 years of W-2s
  • Last 30 days of pay stubs
  • Last 2 months of bank statements (all pages, all accounts)
  • Government-issued ID
  • Social Security number (for the credit pull)

Self-Employed / Business Owners

  • Everything above, plus:
  • Last 2 years of personal tax returns (all pages + schedules)
  • Last 2 years of business tax returns (all pages)
  • Year-to-date profit & loss statement
  • Business bank statements (2–3 months)

The income calculation for self-employed borrowers is based on a 2-year average of your net income after deductions — not your gross revenue. If you've been aggressively writing off expenses, this matters. I always run the income calculation before we go to pre-qualification so there are no surprises.

Pre-Qualification vs. Conditional Approval — Know the Difference

Pre-qualification takes 5 minutes. You give a lender your income and debts, they give you an estimate. No credit pull, no document review. It's not worth much in a competitive market.

Pre-qualification is what sellers and their agents expect in Austin. It means a full credit pull, verified income and assets, and a conditional commitment from the lender. The letter carries weight because it's based on real numbers.

Some lenders blur the line between the two. When I issue a pre-qualification letter, it means the file has been run through automated underwriting and I've reviewed the documents. That's what protects you — and your offer — when things move fast.

Does a Pre-Qualification Hurt Your Credit Score?

A single mortgage inquiry drops your score by 5 points or less — and it's temporary. Most scoring models recover within 60–90 days.

If you're shopping multiple lenders, FICO treats all mortgage inquiries within a 14–45 day window as a single inquiry. So shopping 3 lenders in two weeks has the same credit impact as shopping one. Don't let fear of a credit pull keep you from moving forward.

How Long Is Pre-Qualification Valid?

Pre-qualification letters are typically valid for 60–90 days. After that, the lender needs to re-pull credit and reverify income. If your financial situation hasn't changed, we review the update and issue a new letter. Just flag it with your loan officer before the letter expires and they'll handle it.

One important note: your pre-qualification is based on your financial situation at the time of application. Don't open new credit cards, finance a car, change jobs, or move large sums of money between accounts while under contract. Any of those can change your qualifying numbers and require a re-review.

Why Get Pre-Qualified Before You Start Looking?

Austin moves fast. I've seen homes listed on a Thursday go under contract by Saturday with multiple offers. Buyers who aren't pre-qualified before they find a house are scrambling — and that scramble costs them deals.

Pre-qualification before you search gives you three real advantages:

  1. You know your actual budget. Not an estimate. Not a guess. The number you're approved for, with the rate and payment that comes with it.
  2. Your offer is competitive from day one. A pre-qualification letter with a clean, experienced lender tells the seller you're a serious buyer who can close.
  3. You catch problems early. Credit issues, income documentation gaps, debt ratios — better to surface these before you're under contract and on a 21-day clock.

FAQ: Mortgage Pre-Qualification Timeline

How long does mortgage pre-qualification take?

It depends mainly on whether your file is complete. We review your initial information to discuss pre-qualification and the documents needed for the next step. A self-employed file, a recent credit event, or several income sources depend on how quickly those documents come in.

What documents do I need for mortgage pre-qualification?

For W-2 employees: last 2 years of W-2s, last 30 days of pay stubs, last 2 months of bank statements, and a government-issued ID. Self-employed borrowers also need 2 years of personal and business tax returns plus a year-to-date P&L. Having everything ready before you apply is the single biggest way to speed up the process.

What's the difference between pre-qualification and conditional approval?

Pre-qualification is an initial estimate based on the information you provide. It is not a loan approval or a commitment to lend. Conditional approval requires verification of income, assets, and credit, along with underwriting review. Property and other conditions may remain.

Does getting pre-qualified hurt my credit score?

A mortgage credit check can cause a small, temporary change to your score, and credit scoring models generally treat multiple mortgage inquiries within a short shopping window as a single inquiry. Ask your loan officer whether your pre-qualification review includes a credit pull.

How long is a pre-qualification letter valid?

Pre-qualification letters have an expiration date, and after that your credit and income may need to be updated. If nothing has changed, we review the update and issue a new letter. Contact your loan officer before the letter expires if you’re still actively searching.

Can I get pre-qualified before I find a house?

Yes. Getting pre-qualified before your search gives you a clearer budget and surfaces documentation or credit issues before you’re under contract on a tight timeline. Pre-qualification is not a loan approval or a commitment to lend.

Bottom line: once your documents are in, we review them and issue a pre-qualification letter.
