ITIN Mortgage Loans in Texas: How to Buy a Home Without a Social Security Number

By Adam Styer | NMLS #513013 | July 21, 2026

Almost everything written about ITIN mortgages starts from the same assumption: no Social Security number means no conventional loan, so here's a portfolio product instead. That assumption is wrong, and it's costing people money. Fannie Mae's own guide accepts a valid SSN or an ITIN.

I specialize in files that don't fit the standard box — self-employed borrowers, complicated income, people whose paperwork tells a more complicated story than a W-2. ITIN borrowers land in that category constantly, and they usually arrive having already been told no somewhere else.

Here's what I want you to walk away with: the tax ID is rarely the thing that kills the file. Something else usually is — and it's worth knowing which, because the two situations lead to completely different loans at completely different prices.

First, What an ITIN Actually Is

An ITIN is a nine-digit tax processing number. The IRS issues it to people who need a U.S. taxpayer identification number for federal tax purposes but who don't have, and aren't eligible for, a Social Security number. You apply using Form W-7, submitted with documents that prove both your identity and your foreign status.

The IRS is blunt about the boundaries. An ITIN does not authorize you to work legally in the U.S., does not qualify you for Social Security benefits, and does not "provide or change immigration status." It is a number for paying taxes. Nothing about it is a statement regarding your right to be here — in either direction.

That last point matters more than people realize, because it means a lender can't read your status off your ITIN. They have to look at your actual documentation. Which brings us to the part almost every article on this topic gets wrong.

The Thing Most ITIN Articles Get Wrong

Search "ITIN mortgage" and you'll be told, over and over, that agency financing is off the table and your only option is a specialty portfolio loan at a higher price.

Go read the guideline. The Fannie Mae Selling Guide, section B2-2-01, says: "Fannie Mae requires that each borrower have a valid Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)." Or. Not "and." An ITIN satisfies that requirement on its face.

Keep going and it gets better. Under B2-2-02, Fannie Mae purchases and securitizes mortgages made to non-U.S. citizens who are lawful permanent or non-permanent residents "under the same terms that are available to U.S. citizens." Same terms. Same down payment options. Same pricing structure. No specialty surcharge for not being born here.

I've had people sit across from me genuinely stunned by that, because three lenders already told them otherwise. Some of those lenders were being lazy. Some simply don't originate these files often enough to know the guideline.

So What Actually Gates the File?

Lawful presence. That's the real gate.

Fannie Mae doesn't publish a checklist of acceptable immigration documents. B2-2-02 says the lender "must make a determination of the non-U.S. citizen's status based on the circumstances of the individual case, using documentation it deems appropriate," and then represents and warrants to Fannie Mae that the borrower is legally present.

Read that again, because it has a real consequence for you as a borrower: this is a lender-by-lender judgment call. The guideline delegates the decision. That means two lenders looking at the identical file can reach different answers, and one "no" is genuinely not the market's answer — it's one shop's risk appetite. When I'm working a file like this across 40+ wholesale lenders, that variation is the entire advantage. Same borrower, same documents, materially different outcomes.

If lawful presence can be documented and the rest of the file stands up, you may be a conventional borrower who was misfiled into a specialty product. Worth checking before you accept a higher-priced loan.

When a Portfolio ITIN Program Is the Right Answer

Sometimes agency financing genuinely doesn't fit. When it doesn't, portfolio ITIN programs exist — loans the lender keeps on its own books instead of selling, which is exactly why the guidelines can flex where agency guidelines can't.

The trade is honest and you should hear it plainly: expect a larger down payment and pricing above agency. The lender is holding that risk instead of passing it off, and they price accordingly. Terms vary a lot between lenders, and the spread between the first quote and the fourth is often the most expensive thing about the loan.

One thing that does not flex, on any of these: the lender still has to prove you can repay. The CFPB's ability-to-repay rule at 12 CFR 1026.43 requires a creditor to make a reasonable, good-faith determination of repayment ability — considering income or assets, employment status, the monthly payment, other simultaneous loans, mortgage-related obligations, other debts, residual income, and credit history — and to verify the income or assets relied on using third-party records. Non-QM does not mean no-documentation. It means the documentation can look different from a pay stub. Anyone selling you a "no proof needed" ITIN loan is describing something the rule doesn't permit.

What to Have Ready

The file itself is less exotic than people expect:

  • Your ITIN assignment letter or evidence the number is current.
  • Government-issued photo ID and documentation of your status.
  • Two years of filed federal returns showing the ITIN in use. If you're self-employed, this is where the real work happens — see how underwriters rebuild self-employed income, because write-offs shrink your qualifying number whether or not you have an SSN.
  • Two months of asset statements, with any large deposit sourced. Cash that appears without a paper trail can't be used. Get money into the bank early and document where it came from.
  • Credit. Many ITIN holders have thin files. Alternative credit — rent, utilities, insurance, phone — is often acceptable, but it needs twelve months of history and it has to be built before you shop, not assembled mid-escrow.

The Expiration Trap

This is the one that ambushes people, so I'll be specific. The IRS expires an ITIN that hasn't been used on a U.S. federal tax return for three consecutive tax years — it lapses on December 31 following the third year of non-use. Renewing means another Form W-7 with original or certified identity documents.

If you've been out of the country, or your filing situation changed, or you simply haven't needed to file, your number may already be dead and you wouldn't know. Check it before you write an offer. Discovering it under contract, with an option period running, is a bad week that a five-minute check prevents.

If Your Income Is Also Complicated

A large share of the ITIN borrowers I talk to are self-employed — contractors, restaurant owners, landscapers, trades. That's two structural challenges stacked, and the second one is usually the bigger obstacle. If your returns don't reflect what you really earn, a bank statement loan that qualifies on deposits may fit better than anything driven by tax-return net income, and the broader non-QM menu covers the rest of the ways a strong borrower can look weak on paper. The self-employed mortgage overview is the right starting point if that's you.

Before you accept anyone's no: send me the file. I'll tell you which lane it actually belongs in — agency under B2-2-02, or a portfolio ITIN program — and what each one costs. Those are very different loans, and being sorted into the wrong one is expensive.
