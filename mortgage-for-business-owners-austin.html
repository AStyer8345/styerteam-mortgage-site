Why Do Business Owners Need Specialized Mortgage Planning?

Most mortgage originators are retail order-takers who simply plug your taxable income into an automated underwriting engine. For a business owner, this approach frequently results in unnecessary denials because legitimate business deductions, retained corporate earnings, and entity structures distort what you actually earn.

Proactive mortgage planning evaluates your entire financial architecture. We start with a traditional income analysis, identifying non-cash depreciation, Section 179 expenses, and home office add-backs that increase your qualifying income on conventional loans. If tax write-offs are too steep, we transition seamlessly to bank statement, P&L, or asset depletion programs—giving you complete clarity on purchasing power without conflicting with your CPA's tax minimization strategy.

Illustrative Case • Pre-Filing Tax Coordination Austin (Buda / South Austin), Texas

Austin Construction Company Owner — Tax Planning to $900k Purchase

The Situation: An Austin commercial contracting company owner planned to purchase a $900,000 home in 8 months. In prior years, their CPA aggressively utilized Section 179 vehicle write-offs, leaving net taxable profit under $60,000.

The Planning: Adam met with the owner and their CPA in February before tax filing. Adam demonstrated that by taking standard mileage depreciation rather than full Section 179 write-offs on two trucks, the non-cash depreciation could be added back 100% into qualifying mortgage income.

The Outcome: The owner paid minimal extra tax, increased their documented qualifying income to $280,000/year, and secured a wholesale 30-year conventional fixed mortgage at a prime interest rate, saving over $850/month compared to an emergency alternative-doc loan.

Disclosure: Illustrative scenario based on composite borrower profile. Actual interest rates, loan limits, and qualification guidelines depend on credit profile, property appraisal, and lender criteria.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the questions self-employed borrowers ask most.

When should a business owner start planning for a mortgage?

Ideally 6 to 12 months before you plan to buy. This allows us to review your financial statements before your CPA files annual tax returns, ensuring your deductions and distributions align with your homebuying goals.

Can I use money from my business bank account for my down payment?

Yes! Business funds can be used for your down payment, closing costs, and post-closing reserves. Underwriters typically require your CPA to write a brief letter confirming that withdrawing those funds will not negatively impact day-to-day operations.

What business write-offs can be added back into my qualifying income?

On conventional and jumbo loans, underwriters can add back non-cash paper deductions, including depreciation, Section 179 vehicle/equipment deductions, depletion, amortization, and certain non-recurring business expenses.

What if my business shows declining revenue?

If your revenue declined in the most recent year, conventional underwriters will use the lower year or may require an explanation and updated P&L. If the decline was temporary, Non-QM bank statement loans can evaluate recent trailing 12-month recovery.

Do you work directly with my CPA or financial advisor?

Yes. We frequently collaborate with our clients' CPAs, bookkeepers, and wealth advisors to collect documentation efficiently without taking up your valuable working time.
