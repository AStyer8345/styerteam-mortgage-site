How should your mortgage fit the business?

A mortgage can affect more than a monthly payment. Decide how much liquidity to keep in the business, who owns and can access the funds, and whether an income change or transaction affects the timing. Those decisions should guide the financing review.

For the income calculation and documentation choices, start with self-employed mortgage qualification. Use this page for the related business-planning decisions.

Find your situationCompare the pathsSee an exampleCommon planning questions

Three planning questions to settle early.

How much cash should stay in the business?

Separate your down payment and reserves from payroll, taxes, operating expenses and upcoming business needs. A lender must also review whether business funds are accessible and whether their withdrawal affects operations.

How do ownership and compensation fit together?

Map each entity, your ownership share, wages, distributions and any business debts. Do not change compensation or tax reporting solely for a mortgage before reviewing the effects with your CPA and loan officer.

What will change before closing?

A business sale, new entity, expansion or property purchase can change income and available funds. Bring the dates and expected changes into the discussion before relying on today’s account balances.

Compare the complete path, not just its name.

The easiest-sounding document list is not automatically the best financing choice. Before choosing a program, put the alternatives next to each other:

  1. Does the documented income or asset calculation support the goal?Identify the records the lender will use and the exclusions or adjustments that matter.
  2. How much cash will closing and reserves require?Consider the business’s operating needs as well as personal liquidity. Money in a business account is not automatically available for a home purchase.
  3. What are the total costs and ongoing obligations?Compare written quotes using the same property, loan amount, term and date. Include points, credits, mortgage insurance where applicable and any relevant prepayment terms.
  4. Can the file meet your timing?Identify what is already documented, what needs explanation and which property or business questions remain unresolved.

FAQ — Mortgage for Business Owners in Austin

Some self-employed borrowers may qualify through bank-statement, P&L, 1099, or asset-depletion programs that do not rely primarily on tax returns. Required statements, tax documents, business records, credit, down payment, reserves, and pricing vary by investor and scenario.

Taxable income and mortgage qualifying income can differ. A conventional review considers permitted adjustments, such as eligible add-backs, along with income history and business performance. An alternative method may use eligible deposits, a P&L, 1099 earnings, or assets. Compare the supported calculations before assuming the tax return rules out a suitable loan.

The 2026 baseline conforming loan limit for a one-unit property in Travis County is $832,750, up $26,250 from 2025. No Texas county qualifies as high-cost in 2026, so the same one-unit limit applies statewide. A loan above the applicable conforming limit is a jumbo loan; whether it is a qualified mortgage or Non-QM is a separate classification. Source: FHFA 2026 Conforming Loan Limit Values.

Bank-statement programs may calculate income from eligible deposits after exclusions and an expense treatment. P&L and 1099 programs use their own eligible documents and calculation methods. Coverage periods, preparer requirements, supporting records, credit, leverage, reserves, and pricing vary by investor.

Asset-depletion programs calculate qualifying income from eligible documented assets after required deductions. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac publish specific agency methods; non-QM asset eligibility, discounts, divisors, leverage, reserves, and documentation vary by investor and current program.

A DSCR loan is generally a business-purpose investment-property mortgage that primarily evaluates qualifying rent relative to PITIA. Personal-income, credit, asset, reserve, guaranty, entity, rent, and property-document requirements vary by investor and program. Primary- and second-home borrowers need a different eligible loan type.

K-1 and entity income may qualify after a current analysis of ownership, distributions, business liquidity, income history, and required tax documents. The applicable agency or investor guide determines how income, losses, and eligible adjustments are treated.

A one-time-close structure combines construction and permanent financing at the initial closing. A two-time-close structure uses separate construction and permanent loans. Eligibility, loan size, builder, budget, payment, rate-lock, draw, completion, conversion, title, closing-cost, and legal requirements vary by lender, program, property, and transaction.
