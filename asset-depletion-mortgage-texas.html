Asset Depletion Mortgages in Texas — Qualify on Your Liquid Assets

Retirees, founders post-exit, and high-net-worth borrowers can compare current Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and investor-specific asset-depletion requirements across Texas.

An asset depletion mortgage may let a Texas borrower qualify for a home loan using eligible assets — such as cash, brokerage, or retirement accounts — when employment income is not the best fit for the file. The lender applies the program's eligibility, discount, reserve, and divisor rules to calculate qualifying income. The qualifying-income calculation does not necessarily require withdrawals on the depletion schedule; treatment varies by program.

This is not a loophole. The federal Ability-to-Repay rule (CFPB, 12 CFR §1026.43(c)(2)) requires a lender to verify a borrower's "current or reasonably expected income or assets" before making an owner-occupied mortgage. Asset depletion is the second half of that rule used exactly as written — when your balance sheet is the stronger story, the lender documents assets instead of a paycheck.

Who Asset Depletion Is Built For

  • Retirees with seven-figure portfolios and modest Social Security or pension income who fail conventional DTI on a $1M+ Texas home.
  • Founders post-exit sitting on liquidity from a business sale, equity buyout, or IPO who have no current W-2 income.
  • High-net-worth W-2 borrowers whose total compensation is real but whose base-salary-only DTI calculation kills the file.
  • Trust beneficiaries with substantial assets in a revocable trust but irregular distribution income.
  • Pre-retirees in their late 50s or early 60s who have already left full-time work and live off portfolio income.

How to Review an Asset-Depletion Scenario

Start with current statements and identify account ownership, vesting, access, encumbrances, current distributions, and any funds needed for the transaction or reserves. Then apply only the calculation for an eligible current program.

Fannie Mae's employment-related-assets method and Freddie Mac §5307.1 use different eligibility and calculation rules. Non-QM asset eligibility, discounts, divisors, leverage, reserves, rates, and documents vary by investor and must come from a current written matrix.

A qualifying-income result does not establish a loan amount. Credit, rate, term, property taxes, insurance, association dues, other debts, occupancy, property, and the rest of the program still matter.

Asset Eligibility Is Program-Specific

Cash, depository accounts, securities, retirement accounts, stock compensation, annuities, trust assets, business interests, pledged accounts, and jointly owned accounts can receive different treatment. Eligibility depends on ownership, vesting, access, valuation, encumbrances, taxes or penalties, current distributions, and documentation.

Freddie Mac §5307.1 expressly excludes cryptocurrency from net eligible assets. Treatment of cryptocurrency or converted funds under another program must be verified before moving or liquidating assets.

Common Asset-Depletion Review Issues

1. Seasoning on recent large deposits

If a large balance recently arrived from a business sale, rollover, or another institution, expect to document the source and transfer history. Required account history and documentation vary by program, so review them before moving funds.

2. Pledged assets behind a securities-backed line of credit

A securities-backed line or margin loan can change the eligible value of pledged assets. The program's encumbrance and net-asset rules control.

3. Jointly owned accounts

Joint accounts require review of ownership, access, borrower share, and any program-specific co-owner documentation.

4. "Currently a source of income" double-count problem

Assets already used to support another qualifying income stream may be subject to double-counting restrictions. Review the guide before changing distributions or moving funds.

5. Business operating equity is not liquid

Closely held business interests and business accounts require a different ownership, access, liquidity, and documentation analysis from personal depository or securities accounts.

6. Crypto in any form

Freddie excludes cryptocurrency from the §5307.1 net-eligible-assets calculation. Other programs may treat cryptocurrency or converted funds differently. Confirm eligibility, sourcing, documentation, and timing requirements before taking action.

7. Trust-owned and family-LP assets

Trust and family-partnership assets require review of control, access, valuation, distribution rights, and the complete governing documents.

Quick Answers About Asset Depletion Mortgages

What is an asset depletion mortgage?

An asset depletion mortgage uses eligible assets to calculate qualifying income. It can help retirees, founders, executives, and high-net-worth borrowers whose liquid assets are stronger than their monthly taxable income.

Do assets have to be pledged?

Not always. Asset depletion is often a documentation method, not a pledge. Some private-bank or securities-backed structures may encumber assets, so Adam compares the mortgage structure before the borrower assumes the assets must be moved or pledged.

Which assets may be considered?

Eligible assets may include cash, brokerage accounts, retirement accounts, and certain trust or jointly held assets, depending on ownership, access, seasoning, and lender rules. Crypto and illiquid private holdings are more restricted and must be reviewed carefully.
