Asset Utilization Mortgages in Texas

Asset utilization, sometimes called asset depletion, uses eligible savings, investments or retirement accounts to calculate qualifying income for a mortgage. You can usually keep your investments in place.

Private lender options

Private lenders may accept bank or brokerage assets that agency programs often exclude. That can help if you’ve retired, sold a business or hold much of your money in investments.

Current agency requirements
CriteriaFannie MaeFreddie Mac
Loan type Purchase or limited cash-out refinance of a primary or second home. Purchase or no-cash-out refinance of a 1–2 unit primary home or second home. Enhanced Relief Refinance has separate rules.
LTV limits 70% LTV, CLTV and HCLTV. Up to 80% when the asset owner is at least 62 at closing. For joint accounts, all owners must be borrowers and the borrower using the income must be at least 62. Generally up to 80% LTV, TLTV and HTLTV.
Retirement Eligible IRA, 401(k), SEP or Keogh. The borrower must be able to request a full withdrawal. If the account already pays distributions, the balance can count only if those payments are too low to qualify. The borrower must solely own a fully vested eligible account and have penalty- and early-tax-free access to the unencumbered balance at the note date. The account can’t already count as income.
Other assets Eligible employee severance or lump-sum retirement payouts, including bank balances from those sources. Bank and brokerage accounts, certain retirement payouts or business-sale proceeds. For bank and brokerage assets, at least one borrower who owns the account must be 62 or older. Joint owners must be borrowers or on title. The borrower needs penalty-free access to the unencumbered balance by the note date.
Income Deduct any full-withdrawal penalty, down payment, closing costs and required reserves. Divide by the amortization term in months. Deduct transaction funds, gifts, borrowed funds and encumbered assets. Divide by 240.

Age 59½ generally affects early-distribution taxes. Each agency checks account access under its own rules.

Freddie Mac’s revised rules become mandatory for settlements on or after February 3, 2027. They remove the bank and brokerage age minimum, change loan eligibility and use a 180-month divisor.

Fannie Mae B3-3.4-06 · Freddie Mac §5307.1(a)–(c) · Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-10 · IRS Publication 590-B

A quick example

Start with $2 million in brokerage assets. Set aside $200,000 for closing and reserves. If a lender counts 90% of the remaining $1.8 million and divides it by 60 months, that’s $27,000 per month in qualifying income. The percentages and months are illustrative.

Common questions

Will I need to sell or move my assets?

Usually, you can keep your investments in place. You’ll still need cash for closing, and some lenders have account requirements.

Can I use an IRA or 401(k)?

Possibly. I’ll check who owns the account, which funds you can access and how the lender treats withdrawal penalties.

Can I use stock awards or private shares?

It depends on vesting, marketability and the lender. I’ll check what the program counts.

What credit score and down payment do I need?

It depends on the loan, the property and the lender.
