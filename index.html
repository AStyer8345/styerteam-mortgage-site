Questions I get

Questions about Austin mortgages

Yes. If you are self-employed in Austin, I can look at 12 or 24 months of bank statements, a 1099-only program, a P&L your CPA prepares, or assets you already have. These are non-QM loans. They start from the money the business brings in, not the income left after write-offs. On the strongest bank-statement purchases, the loan can go to 90% of the value, and the minimum credit score is usually 620 to 660.

A DSCR loan is for a rental. I qualify it on the rent, not your paycheck or tax return. DSCR is the rent divided by the full housing payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any association dues. Many programs want 1.0 or higher. Some allow a ratio below 0.75, and some do not use a ratio. Down payments usually start at 20% to 25%. There is no limit on how many properties you can finance this way, and the property can be held in an LLC.

Asset depletion turns money you already have into a monthly income number. The lender divides the eligible assets by 36, 60, or 84 months, depending on the program. Cash is usually counted in full. Stocks and similar investments are often counted at 80% to 90%. Retirement accounts are often counted at 70% if you are under 59 and a half. I use this when someone has retired, sold a business, or has more saved than a W-2 shows.

If you are self-employed, you get K-1 income, you own rentals, or your savings are stronger than your wages, I look at bank statements, 1099s, a P&L, asset depletion, DSCR, and jumbo loans. In Travis County in 2026, a jumbo loan is one above $832,750. I compare those programs across 40+ capital partners.
