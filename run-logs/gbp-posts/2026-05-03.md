## GBP Post (plain text)

What if the seller paying down your rate is worth more than them dropping the price?

I've been seeing this play out across Austin all spring. Buyers go in asking for a price reduction, leave a chunk of negotiating power on the table, and walk away thinking they won. Meanwhile the same seller probably would have given them more if they'd asked for it differently.

Here's the move. Instead of asking for $8,000 off the price, ask for $8,000 in seller concessions toward a temporary rate buydown. A 2-1 buydown structure lowers your effective rate in year one and year two before settling into your locked rate. The savings show up as real cash in your pocket each month, right when you're stretched thinnest from moving costs, furnishing the place, and figuring out the Texas property tax bill.

Same dollar amount from the seller. Different impact for you. A price reduction trims a small amount off your monthly payment for thirty years. A buydown puts most of that money back in your hand in the first two years, when you actually need it.

The spring market in Austin and the surrounding suburbs is soft enough right now that concessions are back on the table. Sellers are negotiating. Most buyers and even some agents do not ask for it, so the leverage just sits there waiting.

Run the math both ways before you write the offer. Or call me first.
