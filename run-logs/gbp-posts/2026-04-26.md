## GBP Post (plain text)

Last year's tax return shows $42K. Real income? Closer to $200K.

That gap is what kills self-employed buyers in the standard mortgage process. You write off everything you legally can, then a lender treats your taxable income like it's the whole story. Not great when you're trying to buy a house in Austin.

Bank statement loans solve this. Instead of looking at your tax returns, the lender pulls 12 to 24 months of business or personal bank statements and uses your actual deposits to calculate income. The math reflects what you actually make, not what you wrote off.

Who it's for: small business owners, 1099 contractors, real estate investors, freelancers, anyone whose K-1 or Schedule C makes them look poorer than they are.

Two things to know before you go this route. Down payment requirements are typically higher than a standard conventional loan. And rates run a touch higher too, because you're trading paperwork for flexibility.

For most Austin self-employed buyers I work with, the math still pencils out. They get into a home this year instead of waiting two more tax cycles for their reported income to catch up.

If that's you, get the conversation started. Most of this we can sort in 24 hours.
