## GBP Post (plain text)

"Self-employed" and "non-QM" are not the same word. A lot of Austin business owners walk in assuming they are.

Here's how it usually goes. Somebody has run their own shop for years, hears that self-employed people can't get a normal mortgage, and goes straight to a bank statement lender. Sometimes that's the right call. Sometimes they just paid for a product they didn't need.

Being self-employed does not rule you out of conventional financing. If your tax returns show enough income and you have the history, a conventional loan may be on the table. Fannie Mae has guidelines written specifically for self-employed borrowers. Non-QM is for when the returns don't tell the real story. It's not the default setting for anyone with a 1099 or an LLC.

It's also not a credit score thing. Non-QM isn't the bad-credit bucket. It's a different way to document income or assets.

So the first question I ask isn't which loan you want. It's what your returns actually show. Then we run it both ways. Conventional if the numbers support it. Bank statement, P&L, or asset-based if they don't. Same property, same loan amount, written quotes side by side: rate, points, closing costs, reserves, any prepayment charge.

A shop that only does one kind of loan can only show you one door. I'd rather check both before you pick.

Self-employed here in Texas and already been told non-QM is your only option? Send me the scenario. Worst case, you get a second opinion.
