## GBP Post (plain text)

How does a lender give you a mortgage without ever looking at a tax return? That question comes up every week from self-employed folks here in Austin, so here's the honest answer.

A bank statement loan qualifies you off the money that actually lands in your accounts, not the net income your CPA works so hard to shrink. We pull twelve or twenty-four months of statements and average the deposits. That average, after a reasonable expense factor to account for the cost of running your business, becomes the income we use to qualify you.

The 12 versus 24 month choice matters. If your last year was your best year, twelve months tells the strongest story. If your income is seasonal or lumpy, which is real life for a lot of Texas contractors and restaurant owners, twenty-four months smooths out the peaks and valleys so one slow quarter doesn't sink you.

Here's when it beats a conventional loan. If your tax returns show thin net income but your bank balances stay healthy, a conventional underwriter has to use that thin number and your debt-to-income ratio blows up. The bank statement approach uses your real cash flow instead, and suddenly the same house is affordable on paper too.

It's not subprime and it's not a shortcut. It's just built for how business owners actually earn.

Curious what your deposits would qualify you for? Let's run the numbers.
