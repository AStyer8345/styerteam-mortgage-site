## GBP Post (plain text)

Ever look at a self-employed client's tax return and see almost no income, even though their bank account tells a totally different story?

I had a borrower a few months back — runs a small contracting business here in Austin, does great, pulls in real money. His CPA had written off enough that his Schedule C showed barely anything after expenses. Great for his tax bill. Terrible for a conventional loan, where underwriting only cares about what the tax return says you made.

That's exactly what a bank statement loan is built for. Instead of using tax returns to calculate income, we use 12 or 24 months of actual bank deposits. Twelve months usually gets a stronger number since we're averaging over less time, but it also means a higher rate. Twenty-four months smooths things out and can price better if the deposits are consistent. It depends on the file.

We ran his numbers both ways. Swapping the income calculation changed his DTI enough to get him qualified at the price point he actually wanted, based on what his business really brings in, not what a tax strategy made it look like on paper.

If you're self-employed in Texas and conventional keeps saying no because of what your CPA did right, this is the product built for exactly that gap.
