## GBP Post (plain text)

Your RSUs are worth more than your base salary, and the bank still qualified you on the base salary. That happens constantly in Austin.

If you work at one of the big tech shops here, a large chunk of your pay shows up as stock, not wages. Then you go shopping above the Texas conforming limit, roughly $832,750 in most counties this year, and the lender looks at your equity like it isn't there. Suddenly you're house hunting on a fraction of what you actually make.

Here's the part most people never get told. Vested RSUs already sitting in your brokerage count as assets at full value. And on the right program, shares that haven't vested yet can count as income too, as long as you have a real track record of grants vesting and more vesting scheduled ahead of you in the grant agreement. The lender averages your trailing history, trims it back for stock volatility, and works with what's left. That isn't a loophole. It's underwriting that reflects how tech people actually get paid.

The catch is that not every lender runs this, and the ones that do write the rules differently. That's the whole reason I shop a file across a lot of lenders instead of living on one rate sheet.

Priced out on paper but not in real life? Bring the grant agreement. The qualifying number is usually bigger than you think.
