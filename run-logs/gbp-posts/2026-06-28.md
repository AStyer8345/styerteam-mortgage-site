## GBP Post (plain text)

Every spring I watch the same thing play out. A self-employed Austin business owner sits down with their CPA, writes off everything they legally can, and shrinks their tax bill down to almost nothing. Smart move on the tax side. Then summer comes, they find a house, and a bank tells them they don't earn enough to buy it.

That is the self-employed trap. Your CPA's job is to make your income look small. A traditional W-2 lender's job is to use that small number against you. You cannot win both games with the same tax return.

I had a contractor last year who cleared real money, drove a paid-off truck, and kept plenty of cash in the bank. On paper his Schedule C said he barely made a living, and a big bank had already turned him down. So we set the tax returns aside. We qualified him on twelve months of bank statements, looked at his actual deposits, and closed him on a house in Round Rock.

Here is the part most people miss. Self-employed does not mean unqualified. It means a W-2 lender is the wrong tool for the job. Bank statement loans, P&L-only programs, and asset-based qualifying exist for exactly this situation, and I run them every month here in Texas.

If you own your business and a bank has made you feel like a risk, let's talk. Your real income probably tells a very different story than your 1040.
