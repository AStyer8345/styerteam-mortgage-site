## GBP Post (plain text)

Most people think rates come from one place. They don't.

When someone asks me where rates are this week, the honest answer is that they're in a different place at every lender I check. And I check a lot of them. Forty plus, on most files. That's the part of the mortgage business nobody explains.

Here's what's been true the last few weeks in Texas. Rates have been moving. Not falling off a cliff, not spiking, just moving. Bond market gets jumpy after a CPI print, the ten-year shifts, and what was a fair quote on Monday is no longer the best quote on Wednesday. Normal market, frustrating if you're trying to time it.

The mistake I see most often: someone gets a rate quote from their bank, takes it as gospel, and locks. They never find out a wholesale lender on the same loan was meaningfully cheaper that morning. Same credit, same down payment, different shop.

This gets bigger on complicated income files. Self-employed, 1099, bank statement, asset depletion, DSCR. The spread between lenders gets wider as the file gets more nuanced. One bank says no. The next prices it higher than it needs to be. The third one fits like a glove.

Don't ask me what rates are this week. Ask me where they fit your file.
