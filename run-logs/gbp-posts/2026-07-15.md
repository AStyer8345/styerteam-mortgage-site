Non-QM loan. The name alone scares people off. They hear it and picture subprime, 2008, the risky junk that blew up the housing market. That is not what this is.

QM stands for Qualified Mortgage. It is a documentation rulebook the CFPB wrote after 2008, mostly about how a lender verifies your income. A non-QM loan simply sits outside that one specific box. It does not mean no rules, and it does not mean risky. Every lender I place these with still has to prove you can actually repay the loan. That ability-to-repay standard never went anywhere.

So who is it built for? The Austin business owner who writes off everything, so the tax returns show a fraction of what they really bring home. The investor buying a rental who wants to qualify on the property's cash flow instead of pay stubs. Real income, real buyers, money that just does not fit a W-2 shaped box.

The other myth is pricing. Folks assume non-QM automatically means a punishing rate. For a strong borrower with reserves and a real down payment, the gap to a conventional loan is usually smaller than they expect.
