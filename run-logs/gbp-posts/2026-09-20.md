## GBP Post (plain text)

Your rental doesn't have to cover its own payment to get financed. That surprises a lot of Austin investors.

Most people I talk to think a DSCR loan is pass or fail. Rent has to beat the payment, a ratio of 1.0 or better, or the deal is dead. Plenty of lenders run it that way, so I get why. It isn't the only way.

Quick refresher. DSCR compares the rent a property can bring in to the full monthly payment on it: principal, interest, taxes, insurance. Texas property taxes are a big reason a rental that looks fine on a napkin comes in tight. A 0.9 means the rent covers about ninety percent of the payment. Some lenders stop returning your call right there.

We have programs that go below 0.75. There are even no-ratio options with no minimum at all. Credit, equity, property, and documentation still matter, and the trade is usually more of your own money left in the deal. Lower ratio, more equity. I'd rather tell you that up front than after you've fallen in love with a property.

I've owned long-term and short-term rentals myself. A spreadsheet that works and a property that survives a slow month are two different things. Sometimes the smart move is a lower ratio and more equity on a deal you actually believe in.

Got a rental a lender said didn't pencil? Send me the address and the monthly rent. I'll tell you straight what's possible.
