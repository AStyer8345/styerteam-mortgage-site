## GBP Post (plain text)

Last March, a couple sat down at my desk and told me they wanted to be in a house by summer. Their scores were in the high 500s. They had six thousand in credit card balances spread across four cards. There was a medical collection on two of the three bureaus that wasn't even theirs.

I told them they weren't ready. Not yet.

That's the conversation a lot of loan officers won't have. It's easier to run a hard pull, send a denial email, and move on. But if I push someone into a loan they barely qualify for, with PMI that eats their budget, and they end up house-poor by month three, that's not me solving their problem. That's me taking the win and leaving them with the fallout.

So we built a four-month plan. Dispute the medical collection. Pay down two cards strategically, not by interest rate but by utilization. Don't open or close anything else. Same day every month, fifty dollars over the minimum, smaller balance first.

They came back in July. Scores up seventy-eight points. Conventional loan with better pricing across the board, lower payment than the FHA we were looking at in March. We closed in Hays County before school started.

If you're wondering whether your credit is good enough to talk to a lender, that's the conversation. Not "can I qualify today," but "what's the smartest path to the right loan."

Run it with me before you assume the answer is no.
