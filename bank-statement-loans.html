The short answer

What is a bank statement loan?

A bank statement loan lets a self-employed borrower qualify using 12 or 24 months of eligible bank deposits instead of relying primarily on tax returns. The lender still reviews credit, assets, reserves, property, and ability to repay; only the way income is documented changes.

Income documentation

Personal or business statements, with 1099-only and P&L paths available for some profiles.

Typical history

Two years self-employed is standard; some programs consider one year plus related W-2 history.

Credit & down payment

Many programs begin around 660–680 credit. Strong files may have options starting at 10% down.

Worked example

How deposit-based income is calculated

12-month business deposits$240,000
Average monthly deposits$20,000
50% expense factor− $10,000
Qualifying monthly income$10,000

The expense factor changes the result

Underwriters remove ineligible deposits, average what remains, then estimate business expenses. A 50% factor is a common business-account default, but the actual factor depends on the lender, industry, and documentation.

  • Service businesses may receive a 25–35% factor.
  • Mixed businesses commonly use 50%.
  • Material- or inventory-heavy businesses may use 50–70%.
  • Some lenders accept a CPA-supported expense ratio.

Choose the right lookback

12-month vs. 24-month programs

Both can work. The stronger choice depends on whether your income is rising, stable, or seasonal.

12-month statements

  • Best when recent income has risen sharply
  • Often 15% minimum down
  • Typically 3–6 months PITI reserves
  • May price 0.125%–0.375% above a 24-month option

24-month statements

  • Best for stable or seasonal income
  • Some strong-credit options begin at 10% down
  • Often 6+ months PITI reserves
  • Longer history can support baseline Non-QM pricing

Detailed Bank Statement Loan Guide

Explore the deeper underwriting details when you need them. The primary answers above remain visible and indexable.

Why these loans exist and how Non-QM underwriting works

Conventional underwriting generally starts with taxable self-employed income. Legitimate deductions—depreciation, vehicle expenses, retirement contributions, health premiums, and other write-offs—can make the return a poor proxy for cash flow. Bank statement programs use documented deposits to address that gap.

Non-QM is a legal classification, not a quality grade. Under the CFPB's Ability-to-Repay rule (12 CFR 1026.43), lenders still make a reasonable, good-faith repayment determination. See the self-employed mortgage guide for the broader program landscape.

Personal, business, P&L, hybrid, and 1099 income methods

Business statements

Business statements show cash flow before owner draws. Underwriters generally apply an expense factor, remove transfers and non-recurring deposits, and assess consistency.

Personal statements

Personal-account programs may use a smaller expense factor because the business has already paid expenses before the owner transfer. Co-mingled accounts can work, but transfers must be identified to prevent double-counting.

P&L and hybrid

A CPA-prepared profit and loss statement may support the income calculation. Hybrid programs review both deposits and P&L figures and commonly use the lower supported income. Learn about the P&L-only mortgage path.

1099-only

Some lenders use gross 1099 income with a flat 10–20% expense factor when most income is documented on 1099s. These programs commonly request two years of 1099s and current year-to-date earnings. See the 1099-only mortgage guide.

Personal vs. Business Bank Statements

Whether the lender pulls personal or business statements changes the qualifying math significantly.

Business Bank Statements

Show gross business cash flow before owner draws. Underwriters apply an expense factor (default 50%) to estimate net income. This usually qualifies you for the most income because the lender sees the full revenue side of the business. Required documents: business license or DBA filing, business bank statements showing the business name, and entity documents if LLC/S-Corp.

Underwriters look for: consistent monthly deposit volume (consistency matters more than amount), no dramatic month-over-month swings without explanation, and deposit volume that roughly tracks the business size. A "consultant" depositing $80,000 in a single month and $200 in the others will get scrutinized.

Personal Bank Statements

Show income after the business has paid everything else — rent, payroll, materials, taxes. Personal-account programs apply a smaller expense factor (often 0–15%) since the money in the account already represents the owner's take-home. Best for sole proprietors and freelancers who run everything through one personal account.

Personal-statement programs are also the path for co-mingled accounts. Roughly half of self-employed Texans run business and personal money through the same account — the underwriter handles it, but expects clear notation on transfers and large non-business deposits (tax refunds, gifts, etc.).

1099-Only Programs

A separate sub-category. If 90%+ of your income is reported on 1099s (real estate agents, insurance agents, independent contractors), some lenders skip the bank statement review entirely and use gross 1099 income with a flat expense factor of 10–20%. Simpler, faster, and usually friendlier than a full bank statement file. Bring your two most recent years of 1099s and a current YTD earnings letter from the payer. Who Bank Statement Loans Are Built For

1099 Contractors

If you receive 1099s from one or more payers, you're the prototypical bank statement borrower. Tax returns understate your income because you're writing off legitimate business expenses. Deposit-based qualifying captures what you actually earn.

Business Owners (LLC / S-Corp)

Owners taking modest W-2 salaries from their own S-Corp and pulling the rest as distributions get squeezed by conventional underwriting. Business statements show the full revenue picture and qualify you for the home your actual income supports. More for business owners →

Real Estate Agents

Variable commission income, heavy 1099 volume, and aggressive expense write-offs make agents a near-perfect fit. 1099-only programs are often the cleanest path. More for realtors →

Consultants & Service Professionals

Attorneys, financial advisors, designers, marketing consultants — service businesses with low overhead often qualify for the lowest expense factors (25–35%), which means more qualifying income from the same deposits.

Restaurateurs & Retail Owners

Higher expense factor (typically 60–70%) but bank statement loans still beat the alternative for owners whose tax returns are buried under depreciation and inventory write-downs.

Gig Workers & Freelancers

Multiple income sources, irregular deposit patterns, no traditional employer — bank statement loans work where conventional fails. 12-month programs handle ramping income best.

What We'll Need From You

Bank statement loans are documentation-light compared to conventional, but they're not no-doc. Plan on:

  • 12 or 24 months of bank statements — personal, business, or both, depending on which program fits.
  • Government-issued photo ID — driver's license or passport.
  • Proof of self-employment for at least two years — business license, DBA filing, Secretary of State LLC filing, or a CPA letter on letterhead confirming dates.
  • CPA letter (sometimes required) — confirming actual business expense ratio if you want a lower expense factor than the lender's default.
  • Two years of 1099s — if going the 1099-only route.
  • Proof of funds for down payment and reserves — recent statements from any account holding the funds.
  • Entity documents — if you're closing through an LLC or your business is structured as an LLC/S-Corp.

What you won't need: tax returns, W-2s, paystubs, employment verifications, IRS transcripts, or a 4506-T.

Quick Answers About Bank Statement Loans

Who is a bank statement loan for?

A bank statement loan is usually for a self-employed borrower whose deposits support the mortgage better than the taxable income on the return. It can fit business owners, consultants, real estate professionals, contractors, and service providers with strong recurring deposits.

Do bank statement loans ignore all underwriting rules?

No. Lenders still review credit, down payment, reserves, property type, occupancy, deposit history, and ability to repay. The difference is that eligible bank deposits may be used to document income instead of relying only on tax returns.

Are 12-month or 24-month statements better?

A 12-month average can help when the most recent year is stronger. A 24-month average can help when income is stable and the file benefits from a longer track record. Adam reviews both paths when program options allow it.

When is another program better?

If the borrower is paid mostly through 1099s, owns strong liquid assets, or is buying an investment property, a 1099-only, P&L, asset depletion, or DSCR path may be cleaner than a bank statement loan. Run the asset-depletion calculator to see what the numbers look like before deciding.
