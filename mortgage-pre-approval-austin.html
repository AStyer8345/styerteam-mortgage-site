Pre-qualification questions, answered

It mostly depends on how fast you can get me your information.

  • We start with a short conversation about your income, savings and what you want to buy.
  • I run a soft credit check.
  • I tell you exactly which documents to send, and nothing extra.

I review it myself, so you are not waiting in a call center line.

You do not need all of this to start. A conversation and a soft credit check is enough for a first look. When it is time for documents, a regular W-2 job usually means:

  • Your last two pay stubs
  • W-2s for the last two years
  • Two months of bank statements, every page
  • A photo ID
  • Tax returns for the last two years, if your loan needs them

Self-employed? It depends on the loan. A bank statement loan may not need tax returns at all.

Veteran? Add your Certificate of Eligibility or DD-214.

Yes. Most letters are good for 60 to 90 days. But you do not start over.

  • I collect a few updated documents, usually your newest pay stub and bank statement.
  • I refresh your credit if it is needed.
  • You get an updated letter.

So it is worth getting pre-qualified early, even if you are months from buying. If something needs fixing, you find out while there is still time.

  • Pre-qualification is a first estimate based on what you tell me and a soft credit check. It is not a loan approval or a promise to lend.
  • Conditional approval comes after your income, assets and credit are verified and an underwriter has reviewed the file. Conditions on the property and other items can still remain.

The hard inquiry comes later, once you are under contract on a home and we start the loan.

Yes. What I need from you depends on the loan.

  • A regular loan uses your tax returns. Lenders look at the income left after write-offs, not your gross sales.
  • A bank statement loan looks at your deposits instead. It may not need tax returns at all.

I look at both and show you which one gives you more room. More on self-employed mortgages.

It depends on the loan. These are typical starting points, and they vary by lender and program:

  • FHA: 580 with 3.5% down
  • Conventional: 620
  • VA: usually 580 to 620
  • Jumbo: usually 700 to 720

A higher score usually means better pricing. If your score needs work, I will tell you what to fix first.

  1. Your agent helps you find a home and make an offer.
  2. Once the offer is accepted, I add the property to your application and we start the loan. This is when the hard credit inquiry happens.
  3. The lender orders an appraisal.
  4. Your file goes through underwriting.
  5. When every condition is cleared, you get a clear to close.
  6. You sign at the title company and get your keys.

How long that takes depends on the loan, the property, the appraisal, title and your documents.

What to know before you ask for a pre-qualification

Sending a note does not require documents and it is not a credit pull. A formal loan review, and any credit check, is a separate step. Starting the note does not guarantee an approval or a rate.
