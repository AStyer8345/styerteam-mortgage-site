What Are the Texas Section 50(a)(6) Home Equity Rules?

Under Section 50(a)(6) of the Texas Constitution, homeowners enjoy significant legal protections when borrowing against their primary homestead equity. The foundational rule is the 80% Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV) cap: your primary mortgage plus your equity loan or HELOC cannot exceed 80% of your home's appraised fair market value.

Texas law also requires a 12-day consumer disclosure period (the loan cannot close until 12 days after you receive the statutory notice of homeowner rights), restricts fees to no more than 2% of the loan amount, and mandates that closings take place at a licensed title company or attorney office. While traditional retail banks enforce rigid W-2 requirements, wholesale broker Adam Styer provides both conforming 50(a)(6) loans and Non-QM bank statement equity options for self-employed homeowners.

Illustrative Case • Debt Consolidation & Renovation Austin (Mueller), Texas

Mueller Homeowner — $140,000 Equity Access Preserving 3% First Mortgage

The Situation: An Austin homeowner owned a Mueller home appraised at $800,000 with a $450,000 first mortgage locked at a 3.125% interest rate. They wanted $140,000 to remodel their kitchen and pay off $35,000 in credit card balances.

The Dilemma: A retail bank proposed a full cash-out refinance into a new $590,000 first mortgage at 6.75%, which would have surrendered their historic 3.125% rate and increased their monthly mortgage payment by over $1,400/month.

The Wholesale Solution: Adam structured a standalone Texas Section 50(a)(6) fixed-rate second mortgage for $140,000. Combined debt was $590,000 (73.7% CLTV, well within the 80% Texas limit). The homeowner kept their 3.125% first mortgage untouched, eliminated their high-interest credit card debt, and saved $820/month compared to a full refinance.

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Straightforward answers to the questions Central Texas borrowers ask most.

What is the 80% rule for Texas home equity loans?

Under Section 50(a)(6) of the Texas Constitution, the total of all mortgage debt on your primary residence (your first mortgage plus the new home equity loan or HELOC) cannot exceed 80% of the home's appraised fair market value. For example, on a $600,000 home, total allowable debt is $480,000.

Why is there a 12-day waiting period on Texas equity loans?

Texas law mandates a 12-day cooling-off period starting the day you receive the written Section 50(a)(6) notice of homeowner rights. The loan cannot close until at least 12 calendar days have elapsed, giving homeowners time to review terms without pressure.

Can I get a home equity loan if I am self-employed with tax write-offs?

Yes! While conventional retail banks require tax returns that show high net taxable income, we offer Non-QM home equity loans that qualify self-employed borrowers using 12 or 24 months of bank statements.

Where must a Texas home equity loan close?

By law, a Texas Section 50(a)(6) home equity loan can only be closed at the permanent physical office of a licensed title company, an attorney at law, or the lender. It cannot be closed at your kitchen table or via mobile notary.

Can I get a home equity loan on a Texas investment property?

Yes, and investment properties are NOT subject to the Section 50(a)(6) rules! The 80% cap, 12-day cooling-off period, and fee limits apply only to your primary homestead. Equity extraction on Texas rental properties is treated as standard commercial/investor lending.
